> Design output (2026-09-06, revision 5 — **owner not in the loop; every §1 decision is _proposed_ and carries a pending disposition**). Instantiates master-spec §19 "MS-2 — Domain logic" as five sequenced sub-milestones, each with its own implementation plan and its own `dev→main` PR, the way the [MS-1 ingestion design](2026-07-05-ms1-ingestion-design.md) instantiated §19 "MS-1 — Core workflow". The master spec and ADRs remain the design source of truth; this document records the MS-2 *decomposition* and the scope decisions it proposes. It introduces no new architecture except where §1 says so explicitly and marks it a proposed decision — every other mechanism below is fixed by an existing ADR or spec section, cited inline.

Decisions **S2-24, S2-25, S2-26** are new and pending.

**Goal:** every listing **in the §2.2 scoring population** carries a **reproducible** 0–100 `deal_score` computed per [ADR 0011](../../adr/adr-0011-composite-deal-score.md) — the weighted geometric mean `base = Π_k max(s_k, 0.02)^{w_k}` at **price 0.50 · fitness 0.25 · seller 0.15 · availability 0.10**, gated by the three non-compensatory veto caps expressed **on the same [0,1] scale as `base`** (device-managed SMR for an enterprise/NAS buyer → `cap = 0.35`; used/seller-refurb with no returns → `cap = 0.60`; seller trust < 0.50 → `cap = 0.60`), with `deal_score = round_half_up(100 · min(base, cap))` — together with the DR-004 per-subscore explanation payload; thin cohorts (`n_eff < 30`) visibly shrink toward neutral and are marked **provisional**; and the documented cohort-relaxation fallback fires when a cohort is too small (master-spec §19 MS-2 acceptance, line 916). §3.2.1 is the normative statement of the formula and its units.

The owner was not available this session. Every row below is a **proposal**, not a settled requirement: each carries an evidence-backed recommended default, the alternatives rejected, the consequence of adopting it, and an **owner disposition that is still pending**. **The MS-2a plan may not be cut while any disposition in this table is `pending`** — that is review finding SA-001 and this document cannot close it; only the owner can.

An override changes the affected sub-milestone's plan, not this decomposition, unless it moves work across a sub-milestone boundary — in which case this document is revised first (§7).

### 1.1 How to ratify

Reply once, one line per decision, in either form:

- `S2-N: accept` — adopt the recommended default verbatim.
- `S2-N: override — <the alternative or value you want instead>` — the named alternative, or a different constant/threshold.

A decision left unanswered stays `pending` and continues to block the MS-2a plan. Dispositions are recorded back into the **Owner disposition** column of this table (and, where an override changes spec text rather than only MS-2 behavior, opened as OQ22+ through the normal process — see §6).

### 2.2 The scoring population (one predicate, used everywhere)

Review finding SA-009: the round-1 document quantified over "every listing" in the goal, "top-5-source listings" in the scope fence, and "every active listing" in the MS-2d exit. Those are three different sets. **One predicate, `P`, replaces all three** (decision S2-16).

A listing is in `P` when **all three** hold:

1. **Source** — its `source_site` is one of the five MS-1 primary recert sources (WD Recertified, Seagate Recertified, ServerPartDeals, goHardDrive, eBay Browse; master-spec line 906).
2. **Live** — it satisfies `Listing.objects.active()`, which is `delisted_at IS NULL AND (expires_at IS NULL OR expires_at > now())` (verified in `catalog/models/market.py`; the second clause exists because the hourly retention sweep can lag a bounded eBay listing's six-hour window).
3. **Observed** — at least one `offer_snapshot` row exists for it. A listing with no observation has no price at all; it is **out of population**, not neutral-scored. S2-7's neutral path covers a missing *capacity*, never a missing price.

Consequences, stated so the acceptance tests can be written against them:

- **Entering `P`** — a new listing enters on its first snapshot and is scored on the next tick. A delisted listing re-entering through `mark_relisted()`, or an expired listing whose `expires_at` is pushed forward by a fresh snapshot, re-enters and is rescored on the next tick (it has no current decision digest, so §3.4.2 always writes).
- **Leaving `P`** — on delist or expiry the listing's denormalized `current_*` score fields are cleared in the same transaction as the delist/redaction (§2.3). Its historical `listing_score` rows are **not** deleted by leaving `P`; they are governed solely by their own retention class (S2-2a), so bounded eBay rows are deleted by the sweep and `merchant_fact` rows persist for audit.
- **Baselines quantify over a different set.** `cohort_baseline` is built from **observations**, not from members of `P`: the 90-day window of non-eBay `offer_snapshot` rows (S2-2b), *including* rows belonging to listings that have since been delisted or have left `P`. A price observed last month is valid market evidence regardless of whether that offer still exists, and those rows are `merchant_fact` / indefinite so they are lawfully retained. This asymmetry is deliberate; §3.4.1 keeps it consistent by stamping the baseline from observation state, not listing state.

`P` is the population in the goal statement, in the MS-2d exit criterion, and in the MS-2 final acceptance in §3.

#### 2.2.1 The current-offer selector (which snapshot supplies current facts)

Review finding SA-018, blocking; decision S2-22. `P` requires only that **at least one** snapshot exist, and §3.4.1's dependency document lists singular offer facts plus "the `observed_at` of the snapshot they came from" — but revision 3 never said **which** snapshot that is. `offer_snapshot` is an append-only time series with composite primary key `(listing_id, observed_at)` (verified at `catalog/models/market.py:510-555`), and `acquisition/persist.py:74-102` appends one row per observation, so a listing routinely has many. Two conforming implementations could therefore score the same stored listing differently.

**Definition (normative).** `S(listing)`, the **current offer snapshot**, is:

```text
eligible(s)  :=  s.usd_total_landed_price IS NOT NULL          # equivalently: s.fx_rate IS NOT NULL
S(listing)   :=  argmax over { s ∈ listing.snapshots : eligible(s) } of s.observed_at
```

- **Eligibility.** A snapshot with a NULL `fx_rate` has a NULL `usd_total_landed_price` by the existing generated-column NULL-propagation rule (`usd_item_price`'s own comment: a NULL `fx_rate` must propagate to NULL, never be treated as USD). Such a row carries no comparable price, so it is **skipped, not defaulted** — the selector falls through to the newest snapshot that does have a valid FX stamp. `stock_status` is read from the *selected* row, so an unstamped row's stock state is not mixed in from a different observation; mixing fields across rows would make the offer facts describe an offer that never existed.
- **Ties are impossible.** `observed_at` is half of the composite primary key, so a listing cannot hold two snapshots at the same instant. There is no tie rule to specify and none may be invented — an implementation that adds one is describing a schema this project does not have.
- **No eligible snapshot.** If every snapshot has a NULL `fx_rate`, the listing has no comparable price: it is treated exactly as S2-7's neutral price path with `price_basis = "price_unavailable"` and the `fx_unavailable` risk flag, and it contributes nothing to any baseline. It stays in `P` (it *has* a snapshot) and is still scored on fitness, seller, and availability.
- **Out-of-order and late arrivals.** Selection is by `observed_at`, **never** by insertion order, primary-key order, or arrival time. A snapshot inserted late but stamped *older* than the current selection therefore **never displaces it** and cannot regress a current score. It **does** enter the 90-day baseline window as ordinary market evidence, so it changes the cohort — and, per review finding SA-020, detecting that arrival is **not** something `observed_at` can do. The baseline consumes *every* eligible row in the window, not just the newest, so an insertion cursor built on merchant observation time misses exactly this row. §2.2.2 adds the server-assigned `ingested_at` stamp that B1 reads instead. The asymmetry stands and is now completely stated: a late observation is valid history (so it must invalidate the baseline) but not a newer statement about the offer (so it must not move `S(listing)`).
- **Corrected snapshots do not exist in this schema.** There is no update path for an `offer_snapshot` row: the acquisition pipeline only appends (`persist.py:74-102`). A merchant correction arrives as a **new** snapshot at a later `observed_at` and is selected by the ordinary rule. No "supersede", "amend", or "void" concept is defined for snapshots, and MS-2 does not introduce one.
- **Expiry or deletion of the selected row.** The retention sweep deletes bounded snapshots independently of the listing. When the row `S(listing)` names is deleted, the selector re-evaluates to the newest surviving eligible snapshot (or to the no-eligible-snapshot path above), which is a genuine change of input facts — it fires rescore trigger **L8** (§3.4.1). This is the only case in which the current offer facts move *backwards* in time, and it is exactly why it needs its own trigger rather than being folded into L2's fingerprint comparison: the fingerprint would notice, but only on a tick that happened to re-read the listing, whereas L8 is a set operation over the sweep's own deleted keys.

**Tests (MS-2c/MS-2d, live DB):** insert three snapshots with different prices and stock states **out of `observed_at` order** and assert the exact selected row, the resulting `deal_score`, and that inserting an older late arrival produces **no** new `listing_score` row for the offer facts, while the baseline responds exactly as §2.2.2 specifies (a new version when the insert moves a run start, none when it lands inside a run); insert a NULL-`fx_rate` snapshot newest and assert the selector skips it and reads *both* price and `stock_status` from the older eligible row; delete the selected row and assert L8 fires and exactly that listing is rescored against the newest survivor; delete every eligible snapshot and assert the `price_unavailable` neutral path with its flag.

#### 2.2.2 The ingestion stamp (how an insert is detected at all)

Review finding SA-020, blocking; decision S2-24. `observed_at` is **merchant observation time supplied by the caller** (`acquisition/persist.py:74-102` passes the adapter's value into `OfferSnapshot.objects.create`), and the model carries no other creation evidence — no `auto_now_add`, no surrogate sequence, composite PK `(listing_id, observed_at)` (`catalog/models/market.py:510-555`). Revision 4's B1 therefore tested `observed_at > last_evaluated_at`, which is a statement about *when the merchant said the price held*, not about *when the row appeared*. A snapshot backfilled after a source outage, a replay, or an operator import lands with an older `observed_at`, enters the baseline window as evidence, and is invisible to that test forever.

**Definition (normative).**

- **`OfferSnapshot.ingested_at`** — `DateTimeField(db_default=Now(), editable=False)`. The value is assigned **by the database at INSERT**, never by an adapter, a fixture, or `append_snapshot`; a caller-supplied value is a defect and MS-2a's test asserts that passing one does not reach the row. It is a **wall-clock arrival stamp with no semantic meaning** — it is never an input to a score, never enters `D`, and never appears in a digest. It exists only to order arrivals.
- **`OfferSnapshot.ingest_stamp_backfilled`** — `BooleanField(default=False)`. The MS-2a data migration sets `ingested_at := observed_at` and `ingest_stamp_backfilled := true` for every pre-existing row. A backfilled stamp is a reconstruction, not an observation, and it is marked as one so an audit can tell the two apart; every row written after the migration carries `false`. *Rejected alternative:* recording a single migration cutover instant in a settings row instead of a per-row flag — it is one less column, but the fact then lives outside the table it describes and does not survive a partial restore of that table.
- **Index** — `("ingested_at",)`, plus the existing hypertable chunk exclusion on `observed_at`. B1 is a `MAX`/`EXISTS` over this index.

**The high-water mark and its commit-lag margin.** `cohort_baseline_current.ingest_high_water` records how far B1 has consumed. A stamp assigned by `Now()` inside a transaction that commits *later* than a concurrent reader's snapshot would otherwise be skipped forever — the classic monotonic-cursor race, which a `bigserial` shares. The stamp is therefore never advanced to the raw maximum:

```text
ingest_high_water := min( max(ingested_at) visible to this evaluation,
                          now() − INGEST_HIGH_WATER_LAG )
B1 fires  ⇔  EXISTS(eligible snapshot of a member listing with ingested_at > ingest_high_water)
```

`INGEST_HIGH_WATER_LAG` is a constant under S2-6, **proposed 60 s**, chosen to exceed the acquisition pipeline's own per-source persist transaction — a row cannot stay uncommitted longer than the request that wrote it, and MS-1's per-source runs are bounded well below that. The cost of the margin is that a just-arrived snapshot may be picked up one tick later than it could have been; the cost of omitting it is a permanently skipped row.

**B3 is the backstop, not the primary.** Even with the margin, a cursor is an optimization: it can only ever *under*-report. §3.3.4's `price_event_digest` (B3) is a content comparison over the same window and catches anything the cursor missed, at the cost of a scan. B1's job is to keep the common case off that path, not to be the only correctness argument — that division is why SA-020's second half (an insert that leaves `count(E)` unchanged) is closed by making B3 a digest rather than a count.

**Tests (MS-2a for the field, MS-2d for the trigger — SA-020's `suggested_validation`):** assert `append_snapshot` cannot set `ingested_at` and that the database assigns it; assert the migration backfills `ingested_at = observed_at` with the flag set, on rows created before it. Then the reviewer's case, in the two shapes the §3.3.4 event definition distinguishes — in **both** the inserted row's `observed_at` precedes `current.last_evaluated_at`, its price equals its neighbours', and `count(E)` is therefore unchanged, so a count comparison could not tell them apart:

1. **Insert *before* the run's first retained snapshot.** The run's start moves back, so the element's `event_at` and `w_rel` change. Assert B1 fires on `ingested_at`, the recomputed `price_event_digest` **differs**, a **new** baseline version is inserted, and exactly that cohort's members rescore.
2. **Insert *inside* an existing run.** `E` is genuinely unchanged — that is what defining events over price runs rather than over surviving snapshots buys. Assert B1 fires, the cohort is **evaluated**, the recomputed `baseline_digest` and `price_event_digest` are **identical**, **zero** version rows are inserted, **zero** `listing_score` rows are written, and the only writes are `last_evaluated_at` and `ingest_high_water` on the pointer.

A negative control inserts nothing and asserts zero writes anywhere, including the pointer's `ingest_high_water` advancing without dirtying a single cohort.

### 2.3 Compliance boundary and derived-field clearing

Review finding SA-002, blocking. Two obligations, both enforced in code and both tested.

**(a) Source composition of every baseline.** Per S2-2b, `cohort_baseline` is built from non-eBay observations only.

- `CohortBaseline.source_composition` is a JSON object mapping `source_site.normalized_name → {price_event_count, raw_snapshot_count, weight_sum}` (the two grains of §3.3.4, review finding SA-017), written on every baseline version.
- **Invariant:** no key of `source_composition` may name a source whose `retention_class` is in `BOUNDED_RETENTION_CLASSES`, and the 90-day SQL pull (§S2-5) filters those sources out at the query level rather than dropping them in Python — the filter is the enforcement, the composition record is the proof.
- **Test (MS-2c, live DB):** seed a cohort with both eBay and non-eBay observations; assert the baseline version's `price_event_count`, `raw_snapshot_count`, `n_eff` and §3.3.4 `observation_vector` equal those computed from the non-eBay subset alone — element for element, so an eBay `listing_id` reaching the vector is caught rather than averaged away, and that `source_composition` names no eBay source. A second assertion covers the degenerate case: a cohort whose only observations are eBay produces **no baseline at all**, and its eBay listings take the §3.3.2 relaxation ladder and then, if still empty, S2-7's neutral path with `price_basis = "no_cohort_evidence"`.
- Master-spec authority: line 399 (retention and licensing are enforced **in the schema**, not by convention), line 404 (do not build or act on a derived eBay price model), line 710 (eBay Browse ≤ 6 h / delete-on-delist / no derived eBay price model).

**(b) Clearing the denormalized score on Listing.** The S2-1 convenience columns (`current_deal_score`, `current_score_provisional`, `current_scored_at`, and — added in revision 5 — `current_cohort_key`, §2.1) are a copy of an authoritative `listing_score` row. When that authoritative row goes, the copy must go with it, in the same transaction — otherwise an expired eBay score survives its own evidence, which is exactly the exposure the review named.

Three hooks, all of which already exist and are extended rather than invented:

| Trigger | Hook (verified to exist today) | MS-2 change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Listing delisted | `Listing.mark_delisted()` — already atomic and already blanks `REDACTED_CONTENT_FIELDS` in the same transaction for delete-on-delist sources | Clear the four `current_*` columns in that same transaction, for **every** delist regardless of retention class, because a delisted listing has left `P` (§2.2) |
| Bounded listing row kept but expired | `Listing.redact_expired()` — the sweep's redaction path for deletion-exempt rows | Same four columns cleared alongside the content redaction |
| Bounded `listing_score` rows deleted by the sweep | `purge_expired` deletes bounded `RetentionGoverned` rows in **independently selected batches**, and **does not** clear denormalized fields on any parent — verified | `ListingScore` declares a **`clear_parent_denorm(deleted_keys)`** classmethod taking the deleted `(listing_id, scored_at)` pairs, called inside the same per-model transaction as the delete and dispatched the way the sweep already dispatches `deletion_exempt_q()` and `redact_expired()`. **Conditional on the exact current row**, per the rule below — the revision-2 `clear_parent_denorm(listing_ids)` signature cleared by listing id alone and is withdrawn (review finding SA-002) |

**The conditional-clearing rule (review finding SA-002, second half).** Revision 2's hook cleared by listing id, and the reviewer is right that this loses a valid score: a bounded eBay listing whose `expires_at` was pushed forward by a fresh snapshot is still `active()` and still in `P`, while an **older** `listing_score` row of its own has already passed its TTL. Deleting that old row and clearing by listing id would erase the current, valid score whose authoritative row is untouched.

`clear_parent_denorm` therefore clears **only when the deleted batch contains the row the pointer names**. For each deleted `(listing_id, scored_at)` pair it issues one guarded update:

```sql
UPDATE listing
   SET current_deal_score = NULL,
       current_score_provisional = NULL,
       current_scored_at = NULL,
       current_cohort_key = NULL
 WHERE id = %(listing_id)s
   AND current_scored_at = %(scored_at)s
```

**Why clearing `current_cohort_key` is safe for the §3.4.1 old-cohort rule.** That column is the recorded last-scored cohort key, and SA-023's fan-out needs to know which cohort a listing *left*. Clearing it looks like it would lose that. It does not, for two independent reasons, and both are load-bearing: B5 evaluates the membership digest over the **union** of stored cohort keys and live ones (§3.4.1), so a cohort that lost its last member is still compared against the empty projection and dirtied without any per-listing memory; and the column is cleared only when the listing's own authoritative score row is deleted or when it is delisted or redacted — after which `matching/resolver.py` never re-evaluates it, so its cohort-shaping projection cannot change again. The one path outside that argument is an operator editing catalog or extraction data on a delisted listing by hand; §6 records it, and the operator remedy is the targeted `score_refresh --force-cohort` run named in §3.4.1.

The `AND current_scored_at = …` predicate is the whole mechanism. If the pointer names a **newer** surviving row, the predicate is false, no row is updated, and the valid current score is left alone. If it names the deleted row, the pointer is cleared.

**No repointing.** The alternative the review offered — repoint atomically to the newest surviving authoritative row — is **rejected**: `current_*` names the newest row by construction, so every surviving row is strictly older, and repointing would resurrect a superseded decision as the listing's current score. The listing is instead left with no current score and is rescored on the next tick, since §3.4.2 always writes when there is no current decision digest. Clearing is a one-tick gap; repointing would be a silently wrong score of unbounded age.

**Concurrency with the score refresh, stated because SA-002 asked.** Both writers touch `listing_score` and then `listing`, and neither takes a lock on `listing` before the update, so there is no cycle to deadlock on:

- The refresh appends the new `listing_score` row and updates `listing.current_*` in **one** transaction (§3.4.2), so the pointer and its target are never separately visible.
- The sweep's clear is a **single guarded UPDATE with no prior read**, so it is correct under both interleavings without a lock: if the refresh commits first, the pointer names the new row, the predicate is false, and the clear no-ops; if the sweep commits first, the refresh's own update overwrites the NULLs with the new pointer. Either order ends with the pointer naming a live row or NULL, never a deleted one.
- The clear is idempotent, which matters because `purge_expired` selects batches independently and the same listing can appear in more than one batch.

**Tests (MS-2a for the model hooks, MS-2d for the sweep):**

1. **The SA-002 regression, exactly as the reviewer framed it:** seed one eBay listing with an **expired old** `listing_score` row and a **newer unexpired** row that `current_*` points at; run `purge_expired`; assert the old row is deleted **and** `current_deal_score` / `current_score_provisional` / `current_scored_at` still identify the newer row unchanged.
2. Delete the row the pointer actually names: seed an eBay listing whose only (and current) score row is expired; sweep; assert the row is gone **and** all four `current_*` columns are NULL.
3. **Interleaving:** with a deletion batch already selected, write a newer `listing_score` row and repoint `current_*` before the clear runs; assert the clear no-ops and the new pointer survives. Run the clear twice and assert idempotence.
4. Delist a scored listing; assert the `current_*` columns are cleared in the same transaction as the delist mark (assert on the post-commit row, and assert the mark and the clear cannot be observed separately). Delisting clears **unconditionally** — the listing has left `P` entirely, so no surviving row is a valid current score.
5. The `source_composition` tests in (a).
6. A negative control: a `merchant_fact` listing's score row survives a sweep with its `current_*` columns intact.

**This boundary is conservative on purpose and the owner may relax it.** S2-2b records that a less restrictive eBay boundary — for example a bounded, six-hour-expiring, eBay-inclusive aggregate — is an owner/counsel decision, not a design derivation. This document does not infer that permission.

### MS-2b — The pure scoring library

Every quantity below is ADR 0011's, reproduced exactly:

- **Weighting and decay:** `w = 2^(−age_days/30)` (30-day half-life) over a **90-day rolling window**; `n_eff = (Σw)²/Σ(w²)`.
- **Price:** `q` = the **ascending weighted price rank** of this listing's `ln($/TB)` within its cohort, defined exactly in §3.3.4 — `q → 0` is the cheapest end and `q → 1` the most expensive, so `1−q` is the cheapness term; `s_price = λ·(1−q) + (1−λ)·0.5` with **`λ = min(1, n_eff/30)`**. `n_eff < 30` ⇒ `is_provisional = True` — **and nothing else sets it** (§3.2.6, decision S2-23). (The ratified target is 30, not 50 — the ADR's own Decision Outcome records why: a 64-observation cohort reaches only `n_eff ≈ 49` under a 30-day half-life, so a `/50` target would leave narrow real cohorts perpetually provisional.)
- **Seller trust:** Beta-Binomial shrinkage with prior **μ₀ = 0.95, κ = 20**, plus a **Wilson lower bound at z = 1.2816**: `s_seller = 0.6·p_post + 0.4·LB`. No ratings ⇒ the S2-12 policy prior as an **explicit missing-data state** (flagged in the payload, never silently averaged in).
- **Fitness:** `s_fit = 0.5·T + 0.3·W + 0.2·C`. **§3.2.4 gives the exhaustive numeric v1 tables** for all three (decision S2-17); the inputs are:
  - `T` (suitability) from `DriveSpec.media_type`, `market_tier`, `recording_tech`, and — per **OQ16** — `dwpd` for SSDs: the endurance class folds into `T`, and the cohort key stays ADR 0011's four-part key.
  - `W` (warranty) from `ProductVariant.warranty_channel` + `warranty_months`, applied to the **claimed** values and carrying a `warranty_unverified` marker on every MS-2 row: `verification_event` exists as a table, but C.4 states that the manufacturer warranty-status lookup that would populate it "is **not yet scheduled in §7/§19**; the unverified-tier rubric fallback applies until it is." MS-2 does not schedule it. §3.2.4 states exactly what the unverified tier evaluates to.
  - `C` (condition) from `ProductVariant.condition` + `recert_channel`.
- **Availability:** a bounded rubric over the four `StockStatus` values that actually exist (`in_stock` / `out_of_stock` / `preorder` / `unknown`). ADR 0011 fixes only the endpoints — **in-stock 1.0, out-of-stock 0.0**; the `preorder` and `unknown` interior values are selected in §3.2.4 as constants under S2-6 (versioned code, bumped and rescored when changed), not settings rows.
- **Accepted v1 calibration notes carried forward from ADR 0011, not "fixed":** the middle band is mildly generous (a median-priced backordered listing scored 69), the `0.02` subscore floor flattens the expensive tail (a top-decile-priced listing scored 14), and the seller-trust prior is sticky-high by design so the `< 0.50` veto fires only on substantial negative evidence. MS-2 reproduces the ratified model; re-fitting constants against real observations is explicitly out of scope.

#### 3.2.1 Normative composition, vetoes, and units

Review finding SA-005, blocking: the round-1 document put `base` in [0,1], named the caps as `35` and `60`, and then wrote `round(100 · min(base, cap))`. Implemented literally, `min(base, 35)` is always `base` and **no veto can ever bind**. The following supersedes every other statement of the formula in this document.

**All subscores, `base`, and `cap` are dimensionless fractions in [0,1]. Only `deal_score` is on the 0–100 scale.**

```text
s_k        ∈ [0, 1]                      for k ∈ {price, fitness, seller, availability}
base       = Π_k max(s_k, 0.02)^{w_k}    w = {price 0.50, fitness 0.25, seller 0.15, availability 0.10}
cap        = 1.0                          when no veto fires
             min over triggered vetoes:
               device-managed SMR for an enterprise/NAS buyer  → 0.35
               used/seller-refurb with no returns              → 0.60
               seller trust < 0.50 (strict)                    → 0.60
deal_score = round_half_up(100 · min(base, cap))    ∈ {0, …, 100}
```

Veto trigger conditions, per S2-8 (affirmative evidence only): `recording_tech = smr_dm` **and** the cohort's `market_tier ∈ {enterprise, nas}`; `condition ∈ {used, refurbished}` **or** `recert_channel = seller`, **and** `returns_policy = no_returns`; `s_seller < 0.50` strictly.

Storage and payload units:

| Quantity | Storage | Range | Payload `scale` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `s_price`, `s_fit`, `s_seller`, `s_avail` | numeric(6,5) | [0,1] | `unit` |
| `base_score` | numeric(6,5) | [0,1] | `unit` |
| `cap_applied` | numeric(4,3), **nullable** — NULL when no veto fired | [0,1] | `unit` |
| `deal_score` | smallint | 0–100 | `centile` |

`cap_applied` is a fraction, **not** an integer — this is a deliberate change from round 1, where an integer `cap_applied` was half of the unit ambiguity. Every numeric field in the machine payload carries its `scale` tag so a consumer (MS-3) cannot mistake one for the other.

**Rounding is pinned to half-up**, not Python's default banker's rounding: `deal_score = int(Decimal(100 * min(base, cap)).quantize(Decimal("1"), rounding=ROUND_HALF_UP))`. This is a reproducibility requirement, not a preference — a rounding mode that depends on the interpreter's default is not a stored input.

> **Divergence to pin in a fixture.** The ratification prototype in `docs/research/drive-deal-scoring-model-test-results.md:261-270` uses bare Python `round()`, i.e. banker's rounding, with the caps already correctly normalized to `0.35` / `0.60`. The two rules differ **only** at an exact `.5` product. MS-2b carries a fixture at that boundary asserting the half-up result, and the golden archetype fixtures are re-derived under half-up rather than copied from the prototype's output.

**Binding tests (MS-2b):** for each veto in isolation and for all combinations, construct `base` strictly above the cap and assert `deal_score` is exactly `35` or exactly `60`, `cap_applied` is exactly `0.35` or `0.60`, and `cap_reasons` names every veto that fired (not only the binding one). A negative control asserts `cap_applied IS NULL` and `deal_score = round_half_up(100·base)` when no veto fires.

#### 3.2.2 Dollars per TB, with lot quantity

Review finding SA-007, blocking; decision S2-15. Master-spec line 1200 requires `offer_snapshot` `$/TB` to divide by the parsed lot quantity, and the round-1 document omitted it entirely.

```text
total_offered_capacity_tb = capacity_tb_per_unit × quantity
dollars_per_tb            = usd_total_landed_price / total_offered_capacity_tb
```

**Cohort membership uses per-unit capacity, never total.** A 2-pack of 8 TB drives is an 8 TB drive offered twice; it belongs in the 8 TB cohort and competes there on its per-terabyte price. Mixing total capacity into the cohort key would put it in a nonexistent 16 TB bucket.

**Quantity source.** `matching/vocab.py` `extract()` returns `quantity` as an `Attribute[int]` carrying `value`, `confidence`, `layer` (N2) and `source_text`. Its patterns and confidences are, verbatim from the source of truth: `lot of N` @ 0.95, `N-pack` @ 0.90, `qty: N` @ 0.90, `Nx` @ 0.70; the regex bounds `N` to three digits. **No extractor work is needed — only persistence**, which §3.3.3 adds. No adapter supplies a structured quantity today (`persist.py` writes adapter attrs only: WD `saleable`, ServerPartDeals `sku` / `variant_title`), so the title extraction is currently the sole source; the precedence rule below is forward-looking for the day one does.

**Semantics, exhaustive:**

| Case | `quantity` used | `quantity_basis` | Risk flag | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Structured adapter quantity present | that value | `structured` | — | Higher provenance tier than a title regex |
| Structured and extracted disagree | structured | `structured_over_extracted` | `quantity_conflict` | Both values recorded in `inputs_json`; the merchant's own field wins |
| Extracted, `confidence ≥ QUANTITY_MIN_CONFIDENCE` | extracted | `extracted` | — | Threshold proposed at **0.85**, derived from the source of truth: it admits `lot of N`, `N-pack` and `qty: N`, and rejects the `Nx` pattern at 0.70, which is the one that also matches speed and generation tokens |
| Extracted, `confidence < QUANTITY_MIN_CONFIDENCE` | 1 | `default_single` | `quantity_low_confidence` | A weak signal must not multiply the denominator |
| Extracted value `≤ 0` or `> MAX_LOT_QUANTITY` | 1 | `default_single` | `quantity_invalid` | `MAX_LOT_QUANTITY` proposed at **100**; the regex admits up to 999, and a 3-digit match above 100 is far more likely a capacity or model fragment than a real lot |
| No quantity signal at all | 1 | `default_single` | `quantity_assumed` | The common case; a single-unit assumption is correct for the overwhelming majority of listings and is stated in the payload rather than hidden |
| Capacity unavailable | — | — | `capacity_unavailable` | `dollars_per_tb` is NULL and S2-7's neutral price path applies regardless of quantity |

`QUANTITY_MIN_CONFIDENCE` and `MAX_LOT_QUANTITY` are constants under S2-6 (versioned code, §3.4.3). `quantity`, `quantity_basis`, and the full provenance record (`{value, confidence, layer, source_text}`) are persisted in `inputs_json` and enter the §3.4.1 input digest, so a change of quantity interpretation rescores the listing.

**Tests (MS-2b table-driven, MS-2c against seeded rows):** equivalent 1-pack and N-pack offers at proportional prices produce identical `dollars_per_tb`; explicit `lot of 4` text; absent quantity; a zero and a 999 quantity; conflicting structured vs. extracted; and an `Nx` title asserting the low-confidence path.

#### 3.2.3 `n_eff` and `λ` — the properties that are actually true

Review finding SA-008: the round-1 test strategy required `n_eff` to be **monotonic in observation count**, which is false for `n_eff = (Σw)²/Σ(w²)` with unequal weights. The reviewer's counterexample is correct and becomes a pinned regression fixture rather than a bug to fix.

**Counterexample fixture.** Ten observations at age 90 days (`w = 2^(−3) = 0.125`) give `Σw = 1.25`, `Σw² = 0.15625`, so `n_eff = 1.5625 / 0.15625 = 10.0` exactly. Adding **one** current observation (`w = 1`) gives `Σw = 2.25`, `Σw² = 1.15625`, so `n_eff = 5.0625 / 1.15625 ≈ 4.378`. Adding data **decreased** `n_eff` — correctly, because one recent observation now dominates the weighted evidence. The fixture asserts the decrease.

**The properties MS-2b actually tests:**

1. **Scale invariance** — `n_eff(c·w) = n_eff(w)` for every `c > 0`.
2. **Bounds** — `1 ≤ n_eff ≤ n` for any `n ≥ 1` strictly positive weights.
3. **Equality at uniform weights** — all weights equal ⇒ `n_eff = n` exactly (the fixture above is its own witness at `n = 10`).
4. **Permutation invariance** — `n_eff` does not depend on observation order.
5. **Conditional monotonicity** — adding an observation whose weight equals the current mean weight increases `n_eff` by exactly 1 (this is the intuition the false property was reaching for, stated in a form that holds).
6. **λ boundary** — `λ = min(1, n_eff/30)`: `λ = 1` exactly at `n_eff = 30`, `λ < 1` strictly below, and `λ` is continuous at 30.

Property-generated cases are compared against a deliberately naive reference implementation of the two summations, so the property tests cannot pass by sharing a bug with the implementation.

#### 3.2.4 The v1 fitness and availability rubrics — exhaustive numeric tables

Review finding SA-011, blocking. Revision 2 fixed the component weights and the two ADR-fixed availability endpoints and left `T`, `W`, `C`, the unverified-warranty value, SSD DWPD behavior, and the `preorder` / `unknown` interior values unselected. Calling them "versioned constants" names where they live; it does not choose them, and materially different rankings all satisfied the document. **Decision S2-17 puts the tables below to the owner.** They are seeded from the older research rubric at `docs/research/principled-deal-score-for-hard-drive-listings.md:249-279`, whose categories (`NAS Pro`, `desktop CMR`, `backorder_7d`, `seller refurb w/SMART proof`) predate the current schema; every mapping from a seed category to the current vocabulary is stated, and every value with no seed is justified.

Inputs are read through §3.3.3's precedence ladder, so an input that is unavailable below model grain arrives as its `unknown` state rather than as a missing key. **Every cell has a value; there is no fall-through.**

##### `T` — suitability (weight 0.5 within `s_fit`)

`T` splits on `DriveSpec.media_type`, because the discriminating attribute differs: recording technology for HDDs, endurance class for SSDs (OQ16 folds `dwpd` into `T` rather than into the cohort key).

`T_hdd = TIER_BASE[market_tier] × TECH_FACTOR[recording_tech]`, rounded half-up to 3 dp.

| `TIER_BASE` | value | | `TECH_FACTOR` | value |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `enterprise` | 1.00 | | `cmr` | 1.00 |
| `nas` | 0.90 | | `smr_hm` | 0.90 |
| `surveillance` | 0.80 | | `smr_unknown` | 0.70 |
| `consumer` | 0.55 | | `smr_dm` | 0.36 |
| `unknown` | 0.50 | | `unknown` **or SQL NULL** | 0.90 |

The product form is deliberate: it keeps the two axes the current schema separates from being re-conflated the way the seed rubric conflated them, and it makes every one of the 5 × 6 = **30** combinations derivable rather than hand-entered.

*Seed reproduction, checked cell by cell:* `enterprise·cmr = 1.00` (seed "enterprise / CMR"); `nas·cmr = 0.90` (seed "NAS Pro / CMR" — the current vocabulary has one `nas`, and recert NAS drives are predominantly the Pro-class parts, so `nas` takes 0.90); `surveillance·cmr = 0.80` (seed "NAS / CMR" — surveillance drives are duty-rated but purpose-specific, which is the same standing the seed gave plain NAS); `consumer·cmr = 0.55` (seed "desktop / CMR"); `consumer·smr_dm = 0.198 → 0.198` ≈ seed "consumer / SMR" 0.20, and the `0.36` factor is chosen to land there.

*The one deliberate divergence:* the seed's standalone "recording unknown → 0.50" was a whole-row value that ignored tier. Here it lands per tier, so `unknown·unknown = 0.450` rather than 0.500. The gap is 0.05 on a 0.5-weighted component of a 0.25-weighted subscore — under 0.006 on `base` before the geometric mean flattens it further. Stated because it is a divergence, not hidden; the exact digits are S2-17's to change.

*Note on `recording_tech`:* the column is `null=True` (`catalog/models/identity.py`), so **SQL NULL is a real, reachable state distinct from the `unknown` enum value**. Both take the same 0.90 factor and both raise the `recording_tech_unknown` flag. `smr_dm` at 0.36 is the low end but is *not* itself the veto; the 0.35 cap in §3.2.1 is separate and fires only for `enterprise`/`nas` tiers, per S2-8.

`T_ssd = TIER_BASE[market_tier] × DWPD_FACTOR[dwpd class]`, over `DriveSpec.dwpd` (`DecimalField(6,3)`, nullable). Class boundaries are **half-open, lower bound inclusive**:

| DWPD class | Condition | `DWPD_FACTOR` | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Write-intensive | `dwpd ≥ 3` | 1.00 | The top endurance tier; nothing above it to discount against |
| Mixed-use | `1 ≤ dwpd < 3` | 0.92 | The standard mixed-use band |
| Read-intensive | `0.3 ≤ dwpd < 1` | 0.80 | The modal recert enterprise SSD |
| Client endurance | `0 < dwpd < 0.3` | 0.60 | Consumer-class endurance sold into an enterprise-adjacent market |
| Unknown | `dwpd IS NULL` **or** `dwpd ≤ 0` | 0.80 | Defaults to the **modal** class, not the best one; raises `dwpd_unknown`. Consistent with S2-8: unknown is neither the unsafe value nor a free pass |

That is 5 × 5 = **25** combinations, all defined. `recording_tech` is ignored for SSDs; if a row carries both `media_type = ssd` and a recording tech, the recording tech is recorded in `inputs_json` and does not enter `T`.

`media_type = unknown` (or no `DriveSpec` at all) **uses the HDD table** and raises `media_type_unknown`. Rationale: `recording_tech` is only ever populated for HDDs, so an unknown-media row carrying one is an HDD; and HDDs dominate this catalog, so the HDD path is the modal reading. An SSD misclassified as unknown lands on `TECH_FACTOR[unknown] = 0.90`, close to its read-intensive factor of 0.80 — a bounded error, and a flagged one.

##### `W` — warranty (weight 0.3 within `s_fit`)

Over `ProductVariant.warranty_channel` × `warranty_months` (nullable `PositiveSmallIntegerField`). Bands are chosen to reproduce the seed's year-denominated tiers exactly. **4 × 6 = 24 combinations.**

| channel \ months | `≥ 60` | `36–59` | `24–35` | `12–23` | `1–11` | `0` or NULL |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `manufacturer` | 1.00 | 0.85 | 0.75 | 0.55 | 0.35 | 0.20 |
| `seller` | 0.55 | 0.50 | 0.45 | 0.40 | 0.30 | 0.15 |
| `none` | 0.10 | 0.10 | 0.10 | 0.10 | 0.10 | 0.10 |
| `unknown` | 0.45 | 0.42 | 0.40 | 0.35 | 0.30 | 0.25 |

*Seed reproduction:* `manufacturer ≥60` = 1.00 ("5-year manufacturer"); `manufacturer 36–59` = 0.85 ("3-year manufacturer"); 24–35 = 0.75 ("2-year verified"); 12–23 = 0.55 ("1-year verified"); `seller 1–11` = 0.30 ("90-day seller warranty"); `none` = 0.10, every band. The `none` row ignores months by design — a `none` channel with a month count is contradictory input, and honoring the months would let a bad write inflate the score. The `unknown` row sits between `seller` and `manufacturer`: the term is claimed but the channel is not established.

**What "unverified" now means (a correction to §3.2's revision-2 wording).** Revision 2 said `W` is "stubbed at the unverified tier for all of MS-2", which SA-011 correctly read as leaving the value unselected. The resolution: MS-2 applies the table above to the **claimed** channel and months as persisted on `ProductVariant`, and *every* MS-2 row additionally carries the `warranty_unverified` **risk flag**, because `verification_event` is unscheduled (C.4) and nothing in MS-2 confirms the claim. `W` is therefore a real, varying, reproducible number rather than a constant, and it is honestly labeled. **The flag does not touch `is_provisional`** — revision 3 said it "contributes to the provisional marker", which SA-019 correctly read as making every MS-2 score provisional and colliding with the thin-cohort meaning the acceptance criteria use. §3.2.6 is normative: `is_provisional` is `n_eff < 30` and nothing else, and warranty uncertainty travels as a risk flag. When the manufacturer lookup is scheduled, dropping the flag — or adopting a separate verified table — is a version bump under S2-6, not a silent change.

The modal MS-2 cell is `unknown` × NULL = **0.25**, since both fields default to their unknown states today. That is a deliberate, visible consequence: most listings' fitness is dominated by `T` and `C` until warranty data exists.

Over `ProductVariant.condition` × `recert_channel`. **7 × 4 = 28 combinations.**

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `new` | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 |
| `recertified` | 0.85 | 0.65 | 0.85 | 0.80 |
| `refurbished` | 0.80 | 0.60 | 0.60 | 0.60 |
| `open_box` | 0.80 | 0.75 | 0.80 | 0.78 |

*Seed reproduction:* `new` = 1.00 ("new / sealed"); `recertified × factory` = 0.85 ("manufacturer recert"); `used` = 0.45 ("used pull"); `unknown × unknown` = 0.35 ("unknown"). The seed's "seller refurb **w/SMART proof** → 0.70" has **no current counterpart**: no field records SMART evidence, so `refurbished × seller` takes **0.60**, deliberately below the seed's proven-SMART value. ##### Availability (`s_avail`, weight 0.10 of the composite)

Over the four `StockStatus` values that exist today (`catalog/models/market.py:57-61`). **4 combinations, all defined.**

| `StockStatus` | `s_avail` | Source | Flag |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `in_stock` | **1.00** | ADR 0011, fixed | — |
| `out_of_stock` | **0.00** | ADR 0011, fixed | — |
| `preorder` | **0.30** | Seed rubric "preorder / ETA unknown → 0.30" verbatim | `preorder` |

#### 3.2.5 The seller-rating contract — provider fields to `p_obs`, `n`, `y`

Review finding SA-016, blocking; decision S2-21. ADR 0011's seller math needs `p_obs` and a same-window sample count `n`, and the research source of truth states the shrinkage in exactly those terms: `y = p_obs · n`, `p_post = (α₀ + y)/(α₀ + β₀ + n)` with `μ₀ = 0.95`, `κ = 20`, plus the Wilson lower bound at `z = 1.2816` and `s_seller = 0.6·p_post + 0.4·LB` (`docs/research/principled-deal-score-for-hard-drive-listings.md:115-161`). Revision 3 proposed to *capture* `feedbackPercentage` / `feedbackScore` while the model declared `positive_count` / `total_count` / `rating_percent`, defined no conversion, and left repeated append-only observations unaggregated. All three gaps are closed here.

**(a) Provider-field semantics — and the one part that is conditional.**

`docs/research/principled-deal-score-for-hard-drive-listings.md:101` records the **public, buyer-facing** eBay semantics: the headline percentage under the username is the share of buyers who had a positive experience, and the bracketed score next to the username is how many buyers have left feedback. That is the display surface, not the API field definition, and the repository contains **no eBay Browse response fixture carrying either field** — `acquisition/sources/ebay.py:114-120` extracts the seller username only, so no cassette exercises them.

> **To be confirmed at MS-2e plan time against the eBay Browse API reference and a live cassette** (the same gate §3, MS-2e already applies to return terms): whether `seller.feedbackScore` is a **total** feedback count or a **net** score (positives minus negatives, the classic eBay definition, under which a seller with 100 positives and 3 negatives publishes 97). The contract below is stated conditionally and **must not** be implemented until the disposition is recorded, because the two readings are not interchangeable: a net score understates `n` and, through `y = p_obs·n`, understates the evidence behind a good seller — which under the strict `s_seller < 0.50` veto is a false-cap risk, the error direction S2-8 exists to avoid.
>
> - **If `feedbackScore` is a total count**, `count_basis = "total_count"` and the contract applies as written.
> - **If it is a net score**, `count_basis = "net_score_proxy"`: it is used unchanged as a **conservative lower bound on `n`** (a net score never exceeds the total), the `seller_count_is_proxy` risk flag is raised on every affected score, and the explanation says so. No back-computation of a total from a rounded percentage is permitted — that would manufacture precision the provider never published.
> - **If the field is absent from the `item_summary/search` response entirely**, seller ratings for eBay are `unavailable` and every eBay listing takes the S2-12 no-rating path until a `getItem` call is separately justified on quota grounds.
>
> The MS-2e plan records which branch fired; no other section of this document depends on which one it is.

**(b) Deterministic normalization.** All arithmetic is `Decimal`, never `float` — a percentage arriving as `99.6` must not become `0.9959999999999999`:

| Step | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| `p_obs` | `Decimal(str(feedbackPercentage)) / Decimal(100)`, quantized to **4 decimal places, `ROUND_HALF_UP`** (§3.2.1's pinned mode), then **clamped to `[0, 1]`** |
| `n` | `int(feedbackScore)` when the value is integral; a non-integral numeric is **truncated toward zero**; the result is clamped at `n ≥ 0` |
| `y` | `p_obs × n`, quantized to 4 dp, `ROUND_HALF_UP`. It is **not** rounded to an integer: `y` is a Beta-Binomial pseudo-count, not a count of events |
| Storage | `rating_percent_raw` and `feedback_count_raw` keep the provider's values **verbatim**; `p_obs`, `n`, `y`, `count_basis` and `is_usable` are the normalized values; `raw_payload` FK preserves the response the values came from (DR-006 PII scrubbing applies) |

**(c) Exhaustive input behavior.** `is_usable = false` on the **selected** observation means the S2-12 policy prior applies as an **explicit missing-data state** — flagged in the payload, never silently averaged in — exactly as §3.2's `s_seller` bullet already requires. Review finding SA-016 (second round): revision 4 said that here and then had (d) select the greatest `observed_at` *among usable rows*, so a valid rating followed by a malformed one had two readings — prior, or the older valid rating kept indefinitely. **(d) now selects the latest row regardless of usability, and this table decides what that row means**; there is no stale-last-good path:

| Provider input | `p_obs` | `n` | `is_usable` | Flag | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `99.6`, `5000` | `0.9960` | `5000` | yes | — | The modal case |
| `100`, `3` | `1.0000` | `3` | yes | `seller_thin_feedback` (`n < 10`) | The shrinkage handles it; the flag explains it. This is archetype **D** in `docs/research/drive-deal-scoring-model-test-results.md:80` |
| `99.6`, `0` | `0.9960` | `0` | **no** | `seller_no_rating` | A percentage over zero raters is not evidence. `y = 0` and `n = 0` would give `p_post = μ₀` exactly, i.e. silently the best-case prior — the "pretend the seller is average" failure the research source explicitly warns against — and the Wilson bound divides by `n`, so `LB` is undefined there rather than merely unhelpful |
| percentage present, count missing/NULL | — | — | **no** | `seller_count_unavailable` | `n` is required by both `p_post` and `LB`; there is no defensible default |
| count present, percentage missing/NULL | — | — | **no** | `seller_percent_unavailable` | Same |
| Either field non-numeric or unparseable | — | — | **no** | `seller_rating_malformed` | The raw value is still persisted for diagnosis; the observation is written with `is_usable = false` rather than dropped, so a provider format change is visible in the data rather than only in logs |
| Percentage outside `[0, 100]` | clamped, then normalized | as given | yes | `seller_percent_clamped` | Clamping is stated rather than treated as impossible; the raw value is preserved |
| Count decreased since the previous observation | as given | as given | yes | `seller_count_decreased` | **Not an error.** Providers do drop feedback (removals, retractions, rolling windows). The newest observation is the provider's current statement and is used as-is; the flag makes the decrease visible without a correction rule this project has no authority to write |

**(d) Selection across repeated observations — the latest row, usable or not, never summed.** `SellerRatingObservation` is append-only, so a seller accumulates one row per genuine rating movement. The seller aggregate consumed by a score is:

```text
aggregate(seller) := the single SellerRatingObservation row with the greatest observed_at
                     for that seller, WHETHER OR NOT it is usable
                     ├─ is_usable = true  ⇒ its p_obs / n / y drive s_seller
                     └─ is_usable = false ⇒ the S2-12 source prior applies, carrying that
                                            row's own flag from table (c) and its observed_at
no SellerRatingObservation row at all ⇒ the S2-12 prior with `seller_no_rating`
```

- **Usability is a property of the selected row, not a selection criterion (review finding SA-016).** Selecting the latest *usable* row would mean a provider format break preserves the last good rating indefinitely, with no age bound, no flag on the score that says the evidence is frozen, and no invalidation when it eventually expires. The alternative — a bounded stale-last-good policy — would need a max age, a decay, a flag and its own expiry semantics, all of which are constants this document would be inventing rather than deriving. **Choosing the latest row unconditionally makes the current provider state the current answer**, which is the same rule §2.2.1 already applies to offer facts. It fails to the declared missing-data state rather than to remembered evidence, and it cannot manufacture a cap: the priors are 0.60 (eBay) and 0.50 (other) against a **strict** `< 0.50` veto (S2-12), so the fallback lowers a well-rated seller's subscore toward neutral without ever tripping the non-compensatory disqualifier — the S2-8 posture that a cap needs affirmative evidence holds through the transition.
- **A malformed or missing response is still written as a row** (table (c)), so the transition is visible in the data and the dedupe rule below treats an unchanged malformed republication as no news.

- **Never sum or difference deltas.** Each observation is a *cumulative republication* of the provider's current state, not an increment. Differencing two republications would treat a re-poll as new evidence and inflate `n`; summing them would multiply it by the poll count.
- **Never average or combine windows.** Averaging mixes provider windows and makes `s_seller` a function of poll cadence rather than of the seller.
- **Deduplicate identical consecutive values.** MS-2e writes a new row **only** when `(rating_percent_raw, feedback_count_raw)` differs from the seller's newest existing row. An unchanged republication writes nothing, so L4's fan-out ("every listing in `P` for that seller") fires on genuine rating movement rather than on every poll — without this rule L4 would rescore a seller's whole inventory on every tick and reintroduce exactly the write amplification SA-014 removed.
- **No row at all, or a selected row that is not usable** ⇒ the S2-12 per-source `seller_policy_prior`, flagged (`seller_no_rating` when no row exists, otherwise the selected row's own flag from table (c)). Note the S2-12 trap that §6 already records: the "other" prior is `0.50` and the veto is `s_seller < 0.50` **strictly**, so an unrated first-party merchant listing is *not* capped.
- **Deletion or expiry of the selected row** re-runs this selection over the surviving rows — the next-latest row, usable or not, or the prior when none survives — and fans out to every listing of that seller as rescore trigger **L9** (§3.4.1). eBay rows are `ebay_listing_observation`-classed and *do* expire, so this is a routine event, not an exotic one (review finding SA-022).
- **Group 3 of `D` (§3.4.1)** carries the selected observation's `p_obs`, `n`, `y`, `count_basis`, `is_usable`, its flag, and its `observed_at` — the normalized values actually consumed, so a re-derivation does not have to re-run this contract against a raw payload that may have been redacted. When no row is selected, group 3 records the prior and the reason in the same shape, so "the prior applied" is itself a stored input rather than an inferred absence.

**Tests (MS-2b table-driven on the pure normalizer, MS-2e on the adapter — SA-016's `suggested_validation`):** one parametrized case per row of table (c), asserting the exact `p_obs`, `n`, `y`, `is_usable`, `count_basis` and flag set; then, for the usable rows, the exact `p_post`, `LB`, `s_seller`, and whether the `< 0.50` cap fires — including a boundary pair straddling `0.50`. Aggregation cases: three observations for one seller in non-monotonic `observed_at` insertion order (assert the greatest `observed_at` wins, not the last inserted); an identical consecutive republication (assert **zero** new rows and **zero** L4 fan-out); a decreasing count (assert it is used with its flag, not rejected and not differenced); a seller whose only observations are `is_usable = false` (assert the S2-12 prior path).

**The SA-016 transition fixtures, one exact expected state each.** Persist a **valid** rating (`99.6`, `5000`), then a strictly newer row of each unusable kind in turn, and assert in every case the **exact** selected row, `s_seller`, the flag set, and the cap outcome — the point being that the older valid row is *never* the selection:

| Newer row | Selected | `s_seller` | Flags | Cap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `99.6`, `0` (zero count) | the newer row | the S2-12 prior | `seller_no_rating` | none (prior ≥ 0.50, strict veto) |
| percentage present, count missing | the newer row | the S2-12 prior | `seller_count_unavailable` | none |
| non-numeric percentage (malformed) | the newer row | the S2-12 prior | `seller_rating_malformed` | none |
| a newer **valid** row (`98.0`, `5200`) | the newer row | recomputed from it | — | per its value |

Each case also asserts a **new `listing_score` row** for every listing of that seller (L4 fan-out) and that the prior-path rows record the prior in group 3 of `D`, so the transition is reproducible from the score alone. A companion case deletes the selected row and asserts the L9 path of §3.4.1 reselects the next-latest row — again regardless of its usability. The adapter tests are cassette-backed and PII-scrubbed per DR-006.

#### 3.2.6 `is_provisional`, stated once

Review finding SA-019; decision S2-23. Revision 3 defined `is_provisional` from `n_eff < 30` in the price contract and then said in §3.2.4 that `warranty_unverified` "contributes to the provisional marker" — and because `verification_event` is unscheduled (C.4), **every** MS-2 row carries `warranty_unverified`. Read together, every score in MS-2 would be provisional regardless of cohort strength, while §19's acceptance, the MS-2d exit criterion and the MS-2e reporting denominator all read provisional as the thin-cohort signal. Two orthogonal uncertainties had been collapsed into one Boolean.

**Normative, and it supersedes every other statement of the marker in this document:**

```text
is_provisional := (n_eff < 30)
```

Nothing else enters it. In particular `warranty_unverified` is a **risk flag only**; so are `recording_tech_unknown`, `tier_unknown`, `dwpd_unknown`, `stock_unknown`, `shipping_unknown`, `quantity_*`, `capacity_bucket_adhoc`, `seller_*` and `fx_unavailable`. A risk flag qualifies *what was scored*; `is_provisional` qualifies *how much evidence the cohort had*. Both appear in the DR-004 payload and MS-3 renders both.

**`provisional_reasons`** is an ordered, deduplicated list on every score row, present exactly when `is_provisional` is true. It **refines but never widens** the Boolean — a reason may only be emitted on a row that already satisfies `n_eff < 30`:

| Reason | Emitted when | `price_basis` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `thin_cohort` | The listing was ranked against a real cohort that has `0 < n_eff < 30`, whether or not relaxation was exhausted | `cohort` |
| `capacity_unavailable` | No usable capacity, so the listing has no cohort at all and takes S2-7's neutral subscore (§3.3.1) | `capacity_unavailable` |
| `zero_observations` | A cohort key exists but the terminal relaxed cohort has zero usable observations (§3.3.2) | `no_cohort_evidence` |
| `price_unavailable` | No eligible offer snapshot supplies a comparable price (§2.2.1) | `price_unavailable` |

**Why the reasons cannot widen the Boolean.** Each of the three non-`thin_cohort` paths consumes **no cohort evidence**, so the score records `n_eff = 0` and `λ = 0` — S2-7 already fixes `λ = 0` for the neutral path, and recording `n_eff = 0` alongside it is what makes the reason list a strict refinement rather than a second, competing definition. The `cohort_key` is recorded on such a row for auditability **wherever one exists** — for `zero_observations` and `price_unavailable` it is the terminal relaxed key, and for `capacity_unavailable` there is **no key at all**, because §3.3.1 gives a listing with no usable capacity no bucket and therefore no cohort. Revision 4 said the key is "still recorded" without that exception, which contradicted §3.3.1; §3.3.5(c)'s CHECK constraints are now the normative statement of both the key and the baseline FK on these rows (SA-024, decision S2-26).

**`price_unavailable` is the fourth `price_basis` value** and is introduced by §2.2.1's selector; revision 3 named three (`cohort`, `capacity_unavailable`, `no_cohort_evidence`). It is distinct from `capacity_unavailable`: that one has a price and no capacity, this one has a capacity and no comparable price.

**Tests (MS-2b table-driven — SA-019's `suggested_validation`):** all four combinations of `n_eff` above/below 30 × warranty verified/unverified, asserting the exact Boolean, the exact `provisional_reasons` list, the presence of `warranty_unverified` in `risk_flags` independently of the Boolean, and the explanation text. Specifically: `n_eff = 40` with `warranty_unverified` ⇒ `is_provisional = false`, empty `provisional_reasons`, flag present. One case per non-`thin_cohort` reason asserting `n_eff = 0`, `λ = 0` and the matching `price_basis`. **MS-2e adds the denominator test:** the coverage and provisional reporting counts a row once per Boolean, not once per flag, so the reported provisional share is a cohort-strength figure.

### MS-2c — Cohort statistics and relaxation

#### 3.3.1 Cohort key and capacity buckets

**Cohort key** = **capacity bucket · market tier · interface/form-factor · condition** (ADR 0011; four-part for SSDs too, OQ16). Stored as a deterministic string on both `cohort_baseline` and every `listing_score` row, so a historical score names the exact cohort it was scored in (NFR-007).

**Key serialization, pinned** (a versioned constant under S2-6, because the key is a stored value that historical rows must still parse):

```text
cap=<bucket>|tier=<tier>|ifff=<interface>-<form_factor>|cond=<condition>
```

- `<bucket>` is the bucket value at three decimals; a **widened** set is `{b1,b2,b3}` with members sorted ascending and comma-separated with no spaces; an **ad-hoc** bucket carries a leading `~`.
- A **dropped** component is the literal `*` (only `cond` is ever dropped, at step 1). An **unknown** component is the literal `unknown` — a token that matches only other unknowns, never a wildcard (§3.3.2).
- A relaxed tier is its parent token (`high_duty` / `client`).

Example, a step-0 key: `cap=16.000|tier=enterprise|ifff=sata-3.5|cond=recertified`.

Component derivation:

- **Capacity bucket** — from **per-unit** `DriveSpec.capacity_tb` (`DecimalField(max_digits=7, decimal_places=3)`, verified). Exact decimal equality would shatter cohorts, so the value is mapped to the nearest member of an ordered constant tuple `CAPACITY_BUCKETS_TB` within a relative tolerance of **5%**; a value outside every tolerance band becomes its own ad-hoc bucket (its value rounded to 3 dp) and carries the `capacity_bucket_adhoc` flag, so an unexpected capacity is visible rather than silently merged.
  - **`CAPACITY_BUCKETS_TB` is derived, not hand-written.** MS-2c ships a management command that enumerates the distinct `drive_spec.capacity_tb` values in the seeded ADR-0018 catalog and emits the ordered tuple; the generator and its output are committed together under `scoring/versions/` (S2-6), so the bucket set is pinned to an `algorithm_version` and a catalog change that introduces a new capacity is a deliberate version bump, not a silent cohort reshuffle. Hard-coding a list here would be an invention with no source of truth behind it.
  - A listing with no usable capacity has **no bucket** and therefore no cohort: it takes S2-7's neutral price path with `price_basis = "capacity_unavailable"` and never enters the relaxation ladder.
- **Market tier** — `DriveSpec.market_tier` after the §2.1 `MarketTier` migration; `unknown` for a listing below model grain (§3.3.3).
- **Interface / form factor** — canonicalized in `scoring/cohort.py`; a token outside the closed vocabulary canonicalizes to `unknown` rather than forking the cohort.
- **Condition** — `ProductVariant.condition`.

#### 3.3.2 The relaxation ladder — deterministic and total

Review finding SA-004, blocking. The ladder is exactly three steps, **cumulative**, evaluated in order, stopping at the first step whose cohort reaches `n_eff ≥ 30`:

| Step | Name | What it does | Cumulative effect |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | `none` | The exact four-part key | — |
| 1 | `condition` | Drop the condition component entirely | key = capacity · tier · interface/form-factor |
| 2 | `capacity` | Add the **immediately adjacent buckets on both sides** (`i−1` and `i+1`) of the listing's bucket index in `CAPACITY_BUCKETS_TB` | condition still dropped; capacity widened to `{i−1, i, i+1}` |
| 3 | `tier` | Replace the tier with its **parent** per the map below | condition still dropped, capacity still widened, tier widened |

Adjacency is **symmetric — both neighbours, not one**, because `$/TB` is already capacity-normalized and there is no a-priori reason a cheaper-per-TB comparison should come from larger drives rather than smaller. At the ends of the bucket tuple only the one existing neighbour is added. The ladder **does not** widen to ±2: three steps is what ADR 0011 (line 68) and master-spec line 1243 prescribe, and step 4 would be a new mechanism, not a relaxation of an existing one.

**Nearest-bucket assignment and its tie rule (review finding SA-004, second half).** §3.3.1 maps a capacity to the nearest canonical bucket within a 5% relative tolerance. "Nearest" is measured by **relative distance** `d(c, b) = |c − b| / b`, computed on `Decimal` at the field's own three-decimal precision — relative, so it matches the tolerance it is compared against, and exact, so the comparison is reproducible. Tolerance is **inclusive**: `d ≤ 0.05` qualifies. When two canonical buckets both qualify, **the lower bucket wins**. Two reasons, and the second is why it is not arbitrary: it is deterministic and independent of tuple length or iteration order; and dividing the same price by the smaller nominal capacity yields the **higher** `$/TB`, so the listing is placed in the more demanding cohort and its `q` is not flattered. That is the same conservative error direction §3.3.2's `unknown → client` mapping and S2-8's cap posture take.

**Ad-hoc bucket adjacency (review finding SA-004, first half).** A capacity outside every tolerance band becomes an ad-hoc bucket that is **not a member of `CAPACITY_BUCKETS_TB`**, so revision 2's step 2 — "the listing's bucket index `i` in `CAPACITY_BUCKETS_TB`" — had no index to take and the algorithm was undefined. It is defined by **insertion point** instead, which is total over every representable capacity:

- Let `i = bisect_left(CAPACITY_BUCKETS_TB, c)` for the ad-hoc value `c`.
- **Predecessor** = `CAPACITY_BUCKETS_TB[i−1]` when `i > 0`, otherwise none.
- **Successor** = `CAPACITY_BUCKETS_TB[i]` when `i < len(CAPACITY_BUCKETS_TB)`, otherwise none.
- Step 2's widened set is `{predecessor, c, successor}` minus the absent ends — the ad-hoc bucket **always stays in its own widened set**, so the listing never loses its own peers by relaxing.

This is the same shape as the canonical case (`{i−1, i, i+1}`), which is why it needs no separate terminal path: steps 1 and 3 are unaffected by capacity, and step 3's parent-tier map is already total, so an ad-hoc listing reaches the same terminal state with the same `relaxation_exhausted` semantics. An ad-hoc bucket is written into the cohort key with a `~` marker (`cap=~10.000`) so it can never silently merge with a canonical bucket of the same value after a version bump changes the tuple, and it keeps the `capacity_bucket_adhoc` flag through the whole ladder.

**Exact fixture expectations (SA-004's `suggested_validation`).** The fixtures pin their own tuple rather than the generated production one, so a catalog change cannot move the assertions:

```text
FIXTURE_CAPACITY_BUCKETS_TB = (4.000, 8.000, 12.000, 16.000, 18.000, 20.000, 24.000, 26.000)
fixed non-capacity components: tier=enterprise, interface/form=sata-3.5, condition=recertified
```

| Fixture | `c` | Assignment | Step-2 widened set | Terminal key (step 3) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Below all buckets | `2.000` | ad-hoc `~2.000` (`d` to 4.000 = 0.500) ; `i = 0`, no predecessor | `{~2.000, 4.000}` | `cap={~2.000,4.000}\|tier=high_duty\|ifff=sata-3.5\|cond=*` |
| Between buckets | `10.000` | ad-hoc `~10.000` (`d` to 8.000 = 0.250, to 12.000 = 0.167) ; `i = 2` | `{8.000, ~10.000, 12.000}` | `cap={8.000,~10.000,12.000}\|tier=high_duty\|ifff=sata-3.5\|cond=*` |
| Above all buckets | `30.000` | ad-hoc `~30.000` (`d` to 26.000 = 0.154) ; `i = 8`, no successor | `{26.000, ~30.000}` | `cap={26.000,~30.000}\|tier=high_duty\|ifff=sata-3.5\|cond=*` |
| **Equal-distance tie** | `24.960` | `d` to 24.000 = `0.960/24 = 0.040`; `d` to 26.000 = `1.040/26 = 0.040` — **exactly equal, both ≤ 0.05** ⇒ **canonical `24.000`** (lower wins), **not** ad-hoc | `{20.000, 24.000, 26.000}` | `cap={20.000,24.000,26.000}\|tier=high_duty\|ifff=sata-3.5\|cond=*` |

The tie value is constructed, not observed: `24.960` is the unique three-decimal capacity equidistant from `24.000` and `26.000` in relative terms, and at 4% it sits inside the 5% tolerance of both. Real catalog capacities rarely tie, which is exactly why the rule needs a purpose-built fixture rather than a hope that it never fires.

Each of the four fixtures asserts, in full: the assigned bucket token, `capacity_bucket_adhoc`, the step-0/1/2/3 cohort keys in order, and the terminal `relaxation_step`, `relaxation_exhausted`, `n_eff` and `λ`. Two of them are seeded with zero peers at every step so they also cover the exhausted-with-zero-observations path into S2-7 (`price_basis = "no_cohort_evidence"`).

**Parent-tier map** (a versioned constant under S2-6; landed by the §2.1 `MarketTier` migration):

| Tier | Parent | Parent membership |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `enterprise` | `high_duty` | enterprise, nas, surveillance |
| `nas` | `high_duty` | enterprise, nas, surveillance |
| `surveillance` | `high_duty` | enterprise, nas, surveillance |
| `consumer` | `client` | consumer, unknown |
| `unknown` | `client` | consumer, unknown |

The map is **total** (every tier value has exactly one parent) and every parent **strictly widens** (each has ≥ 2 members), so step 3 always does something. `unknown` is grouped with `consumer` deliberately: an unclassified listing has to land somewhere, and putting it with client drives biases the resulting distribution *cheaper*, which raises a genuine enterprise listing's ascending price rank q (§3.3.4) and therefore lowers its score. The error direction is conservative — the same posture S2-8 takes on caps, where a false disqualification costs more than a missed one. Unknown-tier listings also carry a `tier_unknown` risk flag throughout.

**Unknown-value relaxation generally.** An `unknown` component is a literal token at step 0 — it matches only other `unknown`s and never silently matches everything. It is then widened by the ordinary steps: condition disappears at step 1, tier maps to `client` at step 3. Interface/form-factor has no relaxation step and stays as-is, including as `unknown`.

**Terminal behavior (decision S2-14).** If step 3's cohort still has `n_eff < 30`, the ladder is exhausted and:

- the listing is scored against that terminal cohort with its **actual** `n_eff` and `λ = min(1, n_eff/30)`;
- `cohort_baseline.relaxation_step = "tier"` and `relaxation_exhausted = true`;
- `is_provisional = true` — by §3.2.6's single rule, since exhaustion means `n_eff < 30` — with `provisional_reasons = ["thin_cohort"]`, and the explanation states both the relaxation path and the exhaustion.

Only when the terminal cohort has **zero** usable observations does the listing fall to S2-7's neutral subscore, with `price_basis = "no_cohort_evidence"` (distinct from `"capacity_unavailable"`, which never reaches the ladder at all). Under S2-2b's eBay exclusion this path is expected to be common early, which is exactly why it is specified rather than left implicit.

**Recorded on every score:** the step that fired, the resulting cohort key, `n_eff`, `λ`, and `relaxation_exhausted`. A relaxed cohort must be visibly relaxed, never silently substituted.

**Tests (MS-2c, table-driven over seeded fixtures):** both adjacency directions; a listing in the smallest and in the largest bucket (one-sided widening); **the four ad-hoc/tie fixtures tabulated above, each asserting its exact keys and full terminal output**; unknown tier and unknown condition; each of the three steps being the first to reach 30; full exhaustion with a nonzero `n_eff`; and full exhaustion with zero observations. Each case asserts one exact cohort key, `n_eff`, `λ`, `relaxation_step`, and `relaxation_exhausted`.

#### 3.3.4 `q`, the observation vector, and what the baseline must persist

Review finding SA-012, blocking. Revision 2 called `q` a "cheapness percentile" and then applied `1−q`, and stored an unspecified "`ln_price_per_tb` distribution summary … quantile points". Both are fixed here: the direction is stated once and used consistently, and the baseline persists a **sufficient statistic**, not a summary.

**Definition of `q` (normative).** For a listing whose `ln($/TB)` is `y_i`, over the cohort's observation vector `{(y_j, w_j)}`:

```text
q = ( Σ_{j : y_j <  y_i} w_j  +  0.5 · Σ_{j : y_j = y_i} w_j ) / Σ_j w_j     ∈ [0, 1]
s_price = λ·(1 − q) + (1 − λ)·0.5
```

This is an **ascending price rank with half-weight ties**, matching the ratification prototype's `weighted_percentile_rank` at `docs/research/drive-deal-scoring-model-test-results.md:214-241` term for term. A low `q` means cheap, `1−q` is the cheapness the subscore rewards, and `s_price` is monotonically **decreasing** in `q`. The half-weight tie term is not cosmetic: without it, a cohort in which every observation carries the same price yields `q = 0` (strict `<`) or `q = 1` (`≤`) depending on the comparison an implementer happens to pick, i.e. the cheapest or the most expensive possible verdict for an identical listing. With it, that cohort yields exactly `q = 0.5` and `s_price = 0.5` at `λ = 1`, which is the correct answer.

Equality is compared on the **quantized** `ln($/TB)` (6 decimal places, the §3.4.2 canonicalization), so ties are decided by the same value the digest sees and not by raw float identity.

**Membership: the vector is used exactly as stored.** The listing's own observations are in the vector if and only if the listing contributed to it — i.e. for the four non-eBay sources. An eBay listing is scored against the vector **without being inserted into it**, which is not a special case but the direct consequence of S2-2b's exclusion. Two consequences to state rather than discover:

- The cheapest **non-eBay** listing in a cohort gets `q = 0.5 · w_self / Σw`, strictly above 0, never exactly 0. A cheapest **eBay** listing gets `q = 0` exactly. The difference is bounded by half the listing's own weight share and shrinks as the cohort grows; it is the price of the compliance boundary, and it is recorded in the explanation payload alongside `q`.
- `margin_iqr` uses the weighted quartiles of the same vector, so it inherits the same membership rule and the same invariance.

**The weighted quartiles and `margin_iqr`, defined normatively** (they were named in FR-006 and in revision 4's payload but never defined, and §3.4.1 group 4 now has to retain them — review finding SA-015). `q25`, `q50`, `q75` are the weighted quantiles of `ln($/TB)` over the vector, computed by the **inverse of the same weighted rank `q`**: `qP` is the smallest vector value whose cumulative weight share, counting ties at half weight exactly as `q` does, reaches `P`. Using one definition for both directions is what makes "this listing sits at rank `q`, which is `margin_iqr` IQRs below the median" a single consistent statement rather than two rankings that can disagree at a tie.

```text
margin_iqr := (q50 − y_i) / (q75 − q25)          when q75 > q25
              NULL                                when q75 = q25   (degenerate cohort)
```

`y_i` is the listing's own `ln($/TB)`, so a **positive** `margin_iqr` means cheaper than the weighted median, in interquartile ranges. The degenerate case is real — a cohort whose prices are all equal has a zero IQR — and it renders as an explicit "no price spread in this cohort" clause in the explanation rather than as a division by zero or a fabricated 0.0.

**What `cohort_baseline` persists (decision S2-18).** The quantile-point summary is replaced by `CohortBaseline.observation_vector`: a JSON array, sorted ascending by `(ln_price_per_tb, event_at, listing_id)`, whose elements are

```text
[ ln_price_per_tb, w_rel, listing_id, event_at ]
```

where `event_at` is the **price event's own time** as defined below, `w_rel = 2^(−(anchor_event_at − event_at) / 30 d)`, and `anchor_event_at = max(event_at)` over the vector. **The weight is anchored to the newest price *event*, not to `now` and not to the newest raw snapshot** (revision 4 anchored it to `max(observed_at)` over the window, which advances on every poll of every member — review finding SA-021 reaches that anchor as well as the representative, since a moving anchor changes every element's `w_rel` and therefore the digest, on every poll). By the §3.4.1 scale-invariance argument, `w(now) = c(now) · w_rel`, and `q`, `n_eff`, `λ` and the quantiles are all invariant under that common factor — so `w_rel` reproduces every statistic exactly while being **constant between price events**, which is what lets `baseline_digest` be stable and the quiet system write nothing.

This is a sufficient statistic in the strict sense: the empirical weighted CDF at every distinct value is recoverable from it, so an arbitrary listing's exact `q` is computable from the stored row alone. A listing-only rescore under L2, L4 or L5 — a seller rating arriving, a policy prior changing, a provenance correction — therefore reproduces the exact prior `q` **without re-running the 90-day SQL pull**, which is precisely the capability the reviewer found missing.

**One element per price event, not per snapshot (also S2-18).** The vector holds one element per `(listing_id, run of equal usd_total_landed_price)` **in the listing's retained history**, restricted to the runs that reach into the window: the **run-start event** of each price run, defined below. *Rejected alternative:* one element per snapshot — at a 60 s–15 min poll cadence a single unchanged listing would contribute thousands of identical prices over 90 days and would dominate the weighted distribution purely by sitting still, which is a defect in the statistic before it is a storage problem. The prototype's cohorts are one observation per listing (`make_cohort` at `drive-deal-scoring-model-test-results.md`), so a price-event series is the closer reading of the ratified model, not a departure from it.

##### The price-event projection `E` — defined once, used everywhere (review finding SA-017)

Review finding SA-017, blocking. S2-18 moved the vector to the price-event grain while B3 still compared `count(live observations in window)` with `baseline.observation_count`, and `observation_count` itself never said which grain it held. Because `acquisition/persist.py:74-102` appends a snapshot on **every** observation, a stable listing has many snapshots and one price event, so the two terms could never agree: B3 would be true immediately after every baseline computation and the cohort would be perpetually stale, burning a full 90-day pull and recomputation every 60 s. The fix is to define the collapse **once**, give it a name, and make every count that is compared with another count come out of the same definition.

**`E(cohort, window)` — the price-event projection, defined over full history and left-censored into the window (decision S2-25).** Review finding SA-021, blocking. Revision 4 computed the runs **inside** the rolling window, so the representative of a run was "the earliest *surviving in-window* snapshot". Once a long unchanged run straddles the 90-day boundary, its earliest in-window snapshot changes at every poll interval as raw snapshots fall out of the window: `event_at` moves forward, `w_rel` rises, the digest changes, a new baseline version is inserted and the whole cohort is rescored — **at poll cadence, for a listing whose price never moved**. The definition is therefore rebased on the listing's history, where a price change is an event whether or not the window happens to contain its start:

```text
-- runs are computed over the listing's FULL retained eligible snapshot history,
-- not over the window; non-eBay per S2-2b, filtered in SQL
partition by listing_id, order by observed_at ascending
price_run_start(s) := s has no eligible predecessor for its listing
                      OR s.usd_total_landed_price <> lag(usd_total_landed_price)
run(e)             := the maximal equal-price span starting at event e
last_obs(e)        := max(observed_at) of the eligible snapshots in run(e)

E(cohort, window)  := { e : price_run_start(e) AND last_obs(e) ≥ now() − 90 d }
event_at(e)        := e.observed_at          -- the run's TRUE start, never clamped
```

- **Membership is windowed; the event time is not.** A run that started before the window but was still in effect inside it is **carried in, left-censored**: it contributes one element whose `event_at` is its real start, which may be older than 90 days. That is the whole SA-021 fix — raw snapshots leaving the window inside an unchanged run change neither `E` nor any element's value, because the element was never anchored to a surviving snapshot in the first place.
- **The carry-in element is *not* clamped to the window start, and that is deliberate** (S2-25 alternative (b)). Clamping the censored element's time — and its weight — to `now() − 90 d` is the obvious reading of left-censoring, and it is wrong here: the window start moves with every tick while every uncensored element's time does not, so the clamped element's `w_rel` would move alone. A common factor cancels out of `q`, `n_eff` and the quantiles (§3.4.1); a single moving element does not. It would change the statistics and the digest continuously, reintroducing exactly the write amplification this finding is about.
- **What the true start costs, stated.** A price unchanged for 200 days is weighted as 200-day-old evidence (`w_rel ≈ 2^(−200/30) ≈ 0.010` against a same-day event's `1.0`), so it stays in the distribution but contributes little. That is what exponential decay *means* at the price-event grain, and it is not a thin-cohort problem: `n_eff` is scale-invariant (§3.2.3), so a cohort in which **every** price is equally old still reports its full `n_eff`. Only a *mixture* of ages is discounted, which is the intended behavior.
- **The window boundary still ages evidence out.** When a run's last observation falls behind `now() − 90 d` — the listing stopped being polled at that price, was delisted, or its snapshots were deleted — the element leaves `E`. `next_window_exit_at = min over E of last_obs(e) + 90 d` is stored on the version, and B2 reads it (§3.4.1). Revision 4's `oldest_observed_at < now() − 90 d` test cannot survive this definition: a carried-in element is *permanently* older than the window, so that comparison would be true forever and every cohort would be perpetually stale. `oldest_event_at` is retained as a diagnostic only and is never used in a dirtiness comparison — the same discipline `raw_snapshot_count` is already held to.

Equality of `usd_total_landed_price` is compared on the stored `numeric(14,4)` value in SQL, not on a float — this is a **different** comparison from §3.3.4's tie rule, which quantizes `ln($/TB)` to 6 dp so that ties are decided by the value the digest sees. The two must not be conflated: run detection is about *this listing's price changing*, the tie rule is about *two listings landing on the same rank*. `E` is a pure function of the retained eligible snapshot set and one boundary instant, so any two evaluations over the same rows return the same events — which is what makes a digest derived from it comparable with a digest stored from it.

**`price_event_digest` — the B3 stamp (review findings SA-020, SA-021).** `SHA-256` over the canonical JSON (§3.4.2) of `E` as the ordered list of `[listing_id, event_at, usd_total_landed_price]` triples, sorted by `(listing_id, event_at)`. It is stored on every version and recomputed by B3. A **count** cannot do this job: an inserted, deleted or re-bounded event that leaves `|E|` unchanged — an older equal-price backfill before a run start, a deletion that merges two runs, a same-cardinality substitution — moves the digest and does not move the count. That is SA-020's second half, and it is why B3 compares content rather than cardinality.

**Two counts, two grains, both persisted on the baseline version:**

| Field | Grain | Computed as | Used by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `price_event_count` | Price event | `count(E)` | The vector length invariant (`len(observation_vector) == price_event_count`) and the §2.3(a) source-composition record. **B3 compares `price_event_digest`, not this count** (SA-020) — the count remains stored because a length invariant is a cheap corruption check the digest cannot express |
| `raw_snapshot_count` | Raw snapshot | `count(eligible snapshots in the window)` | Diagnostics and the MS-2c size report only — **never** compared against a vector-derived number |

Revision 3's single `observation_count` is **withdrawn**; every occurrence in this document is now one of the two above. `source_composition` (§2.3a) maps `source_site.normalized_name → {price_event_count, raw_snapshot_count, weight_sum}`, so the compliance record states both grains and the eBay-exclusion assertion can be made at either.

**What a deletion does to a price run.** `E` is defined over the **retained** rows, so deleting a snapshot can move a run boundary. Four consequences, stated so they are not discovered in production — note that the first two differ from revision 4 precisely because the run is now computed over history rather than over the window:

- Deleting a **non-boundary** snapshot of a run changes neither `E` nor `price_event_digest`; the cohort stays clean.
- Deleting a run's **first** snapshot moves that run's start to the next surviving snapshot of the same run: `event_at` moves **forward**, `w_rel` rises, `ln_price_per_tb` is unchanged. The digest changes and exactly that cohort refreshes.
- Deleting **every** snapshot of a run merges its neighbours if they carry equal prices — the digest changes and `price_event_count` may fall by more than one, which is the case a count comparison reports as "one deletion" and gets wrong.
- Deleting the snapshots that made `last_obs(e) ≥ now() − 90 d` true drops the element even though its own event survives; the digest changes and `next_window_exit_at` is recomputed.

Under S2-2b the vector holds no eBay observations at all, and the four non-eBay sources are `merchant_fact`, so in practice the only deletions reaching `E` are operator action and, for a source whose class ever becomes bounded, the sweep. The rule is specified anyway, because B3 exists precisely to catch a deletion the design did not anticipate — which is only true now that B3 compares content.

**Size bound, stated because SA-012 asks for one.** Elements per cohort = `Σ over member listings (1 + price changes in the 90-day window)`. Recert prices move on a scale of days to weeks, so the per-listing term is single digits; with the ~10² cohorts this document already assumes at v1, a cohort of ~10² members yields ~10³ elements, i.e. tens of kilobytes of JSON per baseline row and single-digit megabytes across the table. `BASELINE_VECTOR_MAX_ELEMENTS` (a constant under S2-6, proposed at **20 000**) is a guard, not a design assumption: exceeding it fails the cohort's refresh into the §4 per-cohort isolation path with an explicit `baseline_vector_overflow` failure rather than silently truncating a distribution. **MS-2c measures the real distribution of vector sizes and reports it**, so the guard is calibrated against data before MS-2d runs it in production.

**Tests (MS-2b table-driven on the pure ranker, MS-2c on the persisted vector):**

| Case | Vector | Expected |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cheapest member | 4 distinct prices, equal weights | `q = 0.5·0.25 = 0.125`; `s_price = 0.875` at `λ = 1` |
| Most expensive member | same | `q = 0.75 + 0.125 = 0.875`; `s_price = 0.125` |
| Duplicated price | two members share `y`, two do not | both duplicates get the **same** `q`, equal to `(below + 0.5·both weights)/Σw` |
| All equal | every `y` identical | `q = 0.5` exactly; `s_price = 0.5` at `λ = 1` |
| Between quantiles | asymmetric weights | `q` equals a hand-computed reference to 6 dp |
| Non-member (eBay) listing | listing not in the vector | `q = 0` when strictly cheapest; the payload records `in_baseline = false` |

**The reproduction test (MS-2c, live DB) — the one SA-012 actually asked for:** compute a baseline; record every member's `q`; **discard all in-memory observations and drop the process's access to `offer_snapshot` for the assertion** (re-read only `cohort_baseline`); recompute each `q` from the persisted `observation_vector` alone and assert **exact** equality to the recorded values at 6 dp. Then advance the clock one hour and assert the recomputed `q` values are still identical — the SA-014 invariance and the SA-012 sufficiency proved by the same fixture.

**The SA-021 window-boundary test (MS-2c/MS-2d, live DB) — the one the reviewer specified.** Seed one listing with **90 days plus one hour of minute-spaced snapshots at an identical price** (and a handful of peers so the cohort is real). Refresh, then advance the clock **minute by minute across the 90-day boundary**, refreshing at each step. Assert, over the whole crossing: the vector holds **exactly one** element for that listing throughout; its `event_at` and `w_rel` never change; the number of `cohort_baseline` version rows created is **zero**; the number of listings rescored is **zero**. Then change that listing's price once and assert **exactly one** new version and one cohort-wide rescore. The bound asserted is the reviewer's: *versions and rescores are bounded by actual price changes, never by raw snapshot exits*. The same fixture run against revision 4's window-local definition produces one version per minute, which is the regression this test pins.

#### 3.3.5 Baseline versions are append-only (reproducing a historical score)

Review finding SA-015, blocking; decision S2-20. Revision 3 stored **one mutable row per cohort key, upserted**. A later semantic refresh therefore overwrote the `observation_vector` that older `listing_score` rows had been computed from, so an old score's `cohort_baseline` FK pointed at a baseline that was no longer the one whose `baseline_digest` the score stored, and the NFR-007 re-derivation test — "re-derive a historical score from its own `inputs_json` plus `algorithm_version`" — could not compute the historical price subscore at all. `src/hw_radar/catalog/models/market.py:510-555` shows the pattern this project already uses for evidence that must survive: offer evidence is append-only time-series data. Baselines now follow it.

**(a) Versions.** `cohort_baseline` holds one **immutable** row per `(cohort_key, baseline_digest)`, unique together, with its own `computed_at`. A refresh that produces a digest already present for that key **writes nothing and reuses the existing version** — the digest is content-addressed, so an oscillation back to a previously observed distribution costs no row. A refresh that produces a new digest **inserts** a new version. No column of a version row is ever updated.

**(b) The current pointer.** `cohort_baseline_current` holds one row per `cohort_key`: `current_version` FK, `last_evaluated_at`, `last_promoted_at`. Promotion is a single-row update inside the same transaction as the version insert, so the pointer and its target are never separately visible — the same transactional shape §2.3(b) already uses for the score pointer.

*Rejected alternative — an `is_current` boolean on the version row with a partial unique index* (`UNIQUE(cohort_key) WHERE is_current`). It reads cheaper and needs no second table, but promoting a version requires **updating the previously current immutable row** to clear its flag. The row is then no longer write-once, the immutability invariant becomes "immutable except for one column", and every test and gate that asserts it has to carve out an exception a later refactor can widen. The pointer table keeps the constraint absolute — `cohort_baseline` takes no `UPDATE` at all — at the cost of one join on the read path, which is a ~10²-row lookup.

**(c) Every score names its exact version — or declares that it had none.** `ListingScore.cohort_baseline` is a `PROTECT` FK to the version row, and `ListingScore.inputs_json` group 4 (§3.4.1) carries `baseline_digest`. The FK is the *identity*; the digest is the *durable name* that survives even a database migration that renumbers rows.

Review finding SA-024; decision S2-26. Revision 4 called that FK mandatory while §3.2.6 enumerated three `price_basis` values that consume **no** cohort evidence — and §2.3(a) states that a cohort whose only observations are eBay produces **no baseline row at all**, so for those paths there is no version to point at. The relationship is pinned here rather than left to MS-2a to guess:

```sql
-- ListingScore, both constraints enforced in the database
CHECK ( (cohort_baseline_id IS NULL)
        = (price_basis IN ('capacity_unavailable', 'price_unavailable', 'no_cohort_evidence')) )
CHECK ( (cohort_key IS NULL) = (price_basis = 'capacity_unavailable') )
```

- **`price_basis = 'cohort'` ⇒ the FK is NOT NULL**, which is the invariant that matters: a score claiming to be cohort-ranked can never lack the evidence it was ranked against.
- **`cohort_key`** is NULL exactly for `capacity_unavailable`, because §3.3.1 is explicit that a listing with no usable capacity has **no bucket and therefore no cohort key** — revision 4's §3.2.6 sentence "the `cohort_key` is still recorded on such a row for auditability" contradicted that and is corrected: it holds for `price_unavailable` and `no_cohort_evidence` (both of which reach the ladder and end at a real terminal key) and not for `capacity_unavailable`.
- **The eBay-only cohort** (a cohort key whose window holds only eBay observations, so §2.3(a) writes no baseline): the listing runs the full §3.3.2 relaxation ladder, terminates with zero usable observations, and is scored with `price_basis = 'no_cohort_evidence'`, `provisional_reasons = ['zero_observations']`, `n_eff = 0`, `λ = 0`, `cohort_key` = the **terminal relaxed** key, and `cohort_baseline_id = NULL`. It is **not** `price_unavailable`, which §3.2.6 reserves for a listing whose own snapshots supply no comparable price — the two are distinguishable and stay distinguishable.
- **Retention and prune.** These rows carry the ordinary S2-2a class (a neutral score is still a score). Because a NULL FK cites nothing, such a row never protects a baseline version from the §3.3.5(f) prune and never appears in condition 2 — so the neutral paths cannot pin storage. `PROTECT` is unaffected by nullability: it constrains deletion of the referenced row, not the absence of a reference.
- **Invalidation.** A neutral-path row has no baseline to change, so **L1 cannot fire for it**; it re-enters scoring through L2 (its own facts, including the arrival of capacity or an eligible snapshot) and through B5, whose union rule (§3.4.1) makes a cohort key that acquires its first non-eBay observation dirty on the tick it appears. Without that union rule an eBay-only cohort would have no stored version to compare and would never become dirty — this is the second reason B5 is evaluated over stored **and** live keys.

**Tests (MS-2a, live DB — SA-024's `suggested_validation`):** persist one row per neutral `price_basis` value and assert the exact stored FK (NULL), `cohort_key` (NULL only for `capacity_unavailable`), retention class and `expires_at` behavior; assert a `cohort`-basis row with a NULL FK is **rejected by the database**, and so is a `capacity_unavailable` row carrying a FK or a cohort key; prune a version while a neutral row exists for the same cohort key and assert the neutral row does not protect it; then add a non-eBay observation to an eBay-only cohort and assert a baseline version appears and those listings are rescored to `price_basis = 'cohort'`.

**(d) The score is re-derivable without the baseline row at all — and so is its explanation.** This is the substance of the SA-015 fix, and a digest alone would not have been evidence. Round 4 found the fix still partial: the retained values reproduced the *number* while `explanation_json` and `explanation_text` also render the percentile **plus the margin in IQRs** and the source composition (master-spec:1264, FR-006), none of which were retained. §3.4.1 group 4 — renamed the **baseline evidence snapshot** — therefore carries, for every score, the sufficient evidence for its own price subscore **and for its own explanation**:

| Group-4 field | Why it is sufficient rather than merely descriptive |
| --- | --- |
| `q` | The listing's exact ascending weighted price rank as computed at scoring time (6 dp, §3.4.2 quantization) |
| `n_eff` | The cohort's effective sample size, the only cohort statistic `λ` reads |
| `lambda_shrinkage` (`λ`) | Recorded rather than only recomputed, so a `λ` rule change is visible as a mismatch instead of being silently re-applied |
| `ln_price_per_tb` (this listing's own) | The value `q` was computed for; recomputing it from `dollars_per_tb` would depend on the log implementation rather than on stored evidence |
| `w_rel` (this listing's own) | Its weight share in the vector, which is what makes the §3.3.4 non-member asymmetry auditable per row |
| `baseline_digest` | Names the exact version, so an audit can *check* `q` against the vector when the version still exists |
| `price_basis`, `relaxation_step`, `relaxation_exhausted`, `cohort_key` | State which path produced the subscore |
| **`q25`, `q50`, `q75`** (weighted quartiles of `ln($/TB)`, §3.3.4) | The explanation renders a **margin against the cohort's price spread**, not only a rank. The quartiles are properties of the vector, which the score deliberately does not carry, so without them the explanation is unregenerable the moment the cohort moves |
| **`margin_iqr`** | Retained rather than only recomputed from the quartiles, for the same reason `λ` is: a later change to the margin definition then shows up as a mismatch instead of silently re-rendering an old score with new arithmetic. Its NULL (degenerate-IQR) state is retained as NULL, not as zero |
| **`source_composition_rendered`** | Exactly the source-composition facts the explanation states — the ordered list of `{source, price_event_count, weight_share}` for the version's contributing sources (§2.3a), rounded as rendered. It is the rendered projection, not the whole `source_composition` map, so what the text claims and what the row stores cannot drift apart |
| **`in_baseline`** | Whether this listing's own observations are in the vector (§3.3.4); the explanation says so for eBay listings, and the value is what makes the `q = 0` asymmetry auditable per row |

**Why these are the complete set, checked against the renderer rather than asserted.** The completeness test is mechanical and is an MS-2b obligation: `explain.py` may read **only** `D` and the score's own outputs; the MS-2b module boundary gives it no baseline access at all, so a field it needs that group 4 lacks is a failure at authoring time rather than a defect discovered when a cohort changes. Anything the explanation renders that is *not* in this table is either an output already on the row (subscores, caps, flags, `dollars_per_tb`) or a fact in groups 1–3.

With those, `s_price = λ·(1 − q) + (1 − λ)·0.5` is computable from the score row alone, and so is every clause of the explanation, so the reviewer's test passes: **create S1; change cohort membership and refresh the same key; discard the offer snapshots and the current baseline; re-derive S1 from `S1.inputs_json` plus its `algorithm_version` through §3.4.3 dispatch, and assert exact equality of every subscore, `deal_score`, `explanation_json` **and `explanation_text` — including the percentile clause, the margin-in-IQR clause and the source-composition clause** — while `S1.cohort_baseline` still names the original version and `S1`'s stored `baseline_digest` still equals that version's.** The vector remains on the version row for **independent audit** (recompute `q` from the distribution rather than trusting the stored `q`); it is not on the reproduction path.

**(e) Retention class.** A baseline version is a `merchant_fact` aggregate, indefinite, exactly as revision 3's single row was — and lawful for the same and only reason: S2-2b keeps eBay observations out of the vector, so the row names no eBay `listing_id` and embeds no eBay-derived price aggregate (master-spec 399, 404, 710). Making versions append-only **increases** the number of retained aggregates, which strengthens rather than weakens the reason the S2-2b exclusion has to be enforced at the SQL-pull level: an eBay row that leaked into one vector would now be retained indefinitely in every version descended from it. `cohort_baseline_current` holds no observation data and inherits the same class.

**(f) The prune rule.** A version row `V` is **prunable** iff **all** of:

1. `V` is not named by any `cohort_baseline_current.current_version`;
2. no `listing_score` row references `V` — neutral-path rows carry a NULL FK (§3.3.5c, S2-26) and therefore never appear here;
3. `V.computed_at < now() − BASELINE_VERSION_GRACE` (a constant under S2-6, proposed **7 days**) — the grace window keeps a version that a concurrent in-flight refresh has computed against but not yet cited from being deleted underneath it.

Condition 2 is enforced by the **database**, not by the query: the `PROTECT` FK makes the delete fail rather than silently orphan a score, so a prune query with a bug degrades to "deletes nothing" instead of "makes history underivable". The sweep treats a `ProtectedError` as the expected outcome for a still-cited version, counts it, and moves on.

Because bounded eBay `listing_score` rows expire under S2-2a while `merchant_fact` scores do not, in steady state the versions actually reclaimed are (i) those superseded before any score cited them — two semantic changes inside one tick, or a cohort with no members in `P` — and (ii) those whose only citations were eBay rows that have since expired. **A version cited by a `merchant_fact` score is retained for as long as that score is**, which is the correct outcome: it is the evidence the score is audited against.

**(g) The sweep.** Pruning runs **inside the `score-refresh` job** (S2-3), at most once per `BASELINE_PRUNE_INTERVAL` (a constant under S2-6, proposed **1 hour**), after the refresh's own phases, and reports `baseline_versions_pruned` on the `ScoringRun` row (§3.4.4). *Rejected alternative:* folding it into the existing hourly `retention-sweep`. Rejected because that sweep's tested invariant is "deletes only bounded `RetentionGoverned` rows"; deleting `merchant_fact` storage-hygiene rows there would weaken an invariant MS-1 already tests, and it would split ownership of the baseline lifecycle across two jobs. Keeping it in the scoring job leaves exactly one writer for `cohort_baseline`.

**(h) The growth this costs, recomputed under the §3.3.4 event definition (S2-25).** Append-only turns "rewrite one row per cohort" into "insert a row per semantic change per cohort", and §3.3.4 sizes a vector at ~10³ elements, tens of kilobytes. The version-creation rate is therefore the **rate of semantic change**, and revision 5 is the first revision in which that rate is actually bounded by price movement:

| Quantity | Derivation | Order |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Members per cohort / cohorts at v1 | This document's standing v1 assumption | ~10² / ~10² |
| Genuine price changes per member per day | Recert prices move on a scale of days to weeks (§3.3.4) ⇒ ~0.1/day | 10⁻¹ |
| Window exits per cohort per day | Bounded by listings ceasing to be polled; same order or below | ≤ 10¹ |
| **New versions per cohort per day** | members × changes + exits | **~10¹** |
| **Bytes per day across the table** | 10¹ versions × 10² cohorts × ~30 KB | **~10¹ MB/day** |

The numbers match revision 4's, and that is the point worth stating plainly: revision 4 **derived** its estimate from price events while its own definition of `E` created a version per **poll**. At a 60 s–15 min cadence that is 10²–10³ versions per cohort per day — order **10³ MB/day**, two orders above the estimate §4 was carrying, on a system where nothing changed. SA-021 is exactly that gap between the estimate and the mechanism; S2-25 closes it by making the mechanism produce what the estimate assumed. The three bounds still apply in order: the digest is content-addressed, so a no-op refresh writes nothing at all (the SA-014 quiet-system property doing the work here too); the prune rule reclaims uncited versions; and `BASELINE_VECTOR_MAX_ELEMENTS` fails an oversized cohort into the §4 isolation path rather than storing it.

**This is not left to production to discover.** MS-2c already measures the vector-size distribution (S2-18); revision 4 extended that obligation, and revision 5 keeps it unchanged because a corrected derivation is still a derivation: **MS-2c must also report measured version-creation rate and measured bytes per day per cohort, and MS-2d must not be enabled in production until that measurement is recorded against the §4 capacity note and the CT-116 disk alert.** If the measurement exceeds the budget, the named follow-up is to release the `observation_vector` (set it NULL, stamping `vector_compacted_at`) on non-current versions past a retention horizon — the score stays re-derivable because (d) puts the sufficient evidence on the score row, and only the independent-audit path is lost. That change is **not adopted here**: it trades away an audit capability, which is an owner call, and it should be made against a measurement rather than against this estimate.

### MS-2d — Scoring service and the scheduled job

- `ScoringService.refresh()` — the two-phase run of S2-3, wrapped in a **`ScoringRun`** row (§3.4.4) with counts, runtime, and `RunFailureClass` on error, so NFR-004 covers scoring the same way it covers acquisition.

#### 3.4.1 The invalidation model

Review finding SA-003, blocking: the round-1 rule ("a cohort is dirty when a snapshot has `observed_at` later than `baseline.computed_at`", plus grain changes and version bumps) invalidates on **insertions only**, so a score could stay current forever while its inputs expired, its window slid, its seller's rating changed, or its refdata was corrected.

The replacement has two halves: **which baselines are stale**, and **which listings must be rescored**.

**A. Baseline staleness.** A cohort's **current baseline version** (§3.3.5) is stale — meaning the cohort must be recomputed, which either promotes a newly inserted version or, when the recomputed digest is unchanged, only advances the pointer's `last_evaluated_at` — when **any** of the following holds:

| # | Trigger | Detection (no queue, per S2-4) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| B1 | **A row was inserted** into the cohort's observation set | `EXISTS(eligible offer_snapshot of a member listing with ingested_at > current.ingest_high_water)`, per §2.2.2. **Not `observed_at`** — that is merchant observation time and a backfilled older row evades it entirely (review finding SA-020). The high-water carries the §2.2.2 commit-lag margin, so an in-flight insert is picked up on a later tick rather than skipped |
| B2 | **A price event left the 90-day window** | `now() ≥ current_version.next_window_exit_at`, the stored `min over E of last_obs(e) + 90 d` (§3.3.4). The stored stamp is what makes this detectable **without any new snapshot**, which is the round-1 gap; it replaces revision 4's `oldest_observed_at` test, which the left-censored carry-in of §3.3.4 would make permanently true. This is a real membership change, not a decay effect: an element leaves the vector |
| B3 | **The content of `E` changed at all** — deletion, backfill, run re-bounding | `price_event_digest(E(cohort, window)) ≠ version.price_event_digest` — **both terms are the §3.3.4 price-event projection, hashed by the same rule** (review findings SA-017, SA-020). Revision 4 compared `count(E)`, which cannot see an insert or deletion that leaves the cardinality unchanged; a digest can. `raw_snapshot_count` remains barred from every dirtiness comparison |
| B4 | Cohort-attribute / refdata change | any `drive_spec`, `product_model`, or `product_variant` row reachable from a member listing has `updated_at > current.last_evaluated_at` (all three are `TimeStamped`, verified at `catalog/models/identity.py:126,163,200`) |
| B5 | **Membership or member-projection change** | the recomputed `member_projection_digest` differs from the current version's — evaluated over the **union** of stored and live cohort keys (below). This catches a same-grain re-resolution target change, a listing moving between cohorts because its **extracted** attributes changed, and a cohort losing its last member (review finding SA-023) |
| B6 | `algorithm_version` bump | `current_version.algorithm_version ≠ ALGORITHM_VERSION` |

Throughout this section, **`current`** is the `cohort_baseline_current` pointer row and **`version`** / `current_version` is the immutable `cohort_baseline` row it names (§3.3.5). Every mutable stamp is on the pointer; every semantic field is on the version.

**There is no time-only staleness trigger, and revision 2's `BASELINE_MAX_AGE` ceiling is withdrawn (review finding SA-014).** Advancing `now` without any dependency change multiplies every weight by one positive constant: `w_i(now) = 2^(−(now − t_i)/30 d) = 2^(−(now − t₀)/30 d) · 2^((t_i − t₀)/30 d) = c(now) · v_i`, where `v_i` depends only on the observation timestamp. Every statistic this design reads from the weight vector is a ratio of weight sums, so every one of them is invariant under that common factor:

- `n_eff = (Σw)²/Σ(w²)` is exactly scale-invariant (§3.2.3 property 1), so `λ = min(1, n_eff/30)` is too;
- the weighted rank `q` (§3.3.4) and the weighted quantiles behind `margin_iqr` are ratios of weight sums against the same total, so they are invariant as well.

Revision 2's claim that "`n_eff` and `λ` would drift on every tick" was therefore false, and B7 was doing no work except forcing a full cohort recomputation every hour and — because the baseline's row identity and `computed_at` were part of the L2 fingerprint — appending a fresh `listing_score` row for every affected listing on an otherwise quiet system. Baselines now refresh only on B1–B6, all of which are real dependency changes, and the incidental identity of a recomputation is kept out of the decision digest by §3.4.1's `baseline_digest` rule below.

**Two stamps, on two tables.** A recomputation that produces the same semantic content must be invisible downstream. Under §3.3.5 the two stamps separate cleanly by mutability:

- **`cohort_baseline.computed_at`** — the immutable creation instant of a **version**. Because a version exists only when its `baseline_digest` is new, "the current version's `computed_at` advanced" and "the semantic content changed" are the same statement, which is what L1 reads. Revision 3 achieved this with a conditional `UPDATE`; versioning makes it structural.
- **`cohort_baseline_current.last_evaluated_at`** — a mutable freshness stamp advanced on **every** evaluation, changed or not, on the pointer row rather than on the evidence. B1, B4 and B5 compare against it, so an unchanged cohort is not re-detected as dirty on the next tick. This mirrors `ListingResolution.last_evaluated_at` verbatim, including its rationale: the evidence describes the verdict, the stamp describes when the verdict was last reconfirmed. Advancing it is a one-row update that touches no observation data, so a quiet tick still writes no evidence.

**`baseline_digest`** is `SHA-256` over the canonical JSON (§3.4.2 canonicalization) of the **semantic** baseline only: `cohort_key`, `relaxation_step`, `relaxation_exhausted`, `window_days`, `half_life_days`, `algorithm_version`, the §3.3.4 `observation_vector`, and `price_event_count` (which is `len(observation_vector)`, so it adds no freedom — it is included so a truncated vector cannot silently share a digest with a complete one). It contains no row id, no `computed_at`, no `last_evaluated_at`, no `ingest_high_water`, no `next_window_exit_at` and **not** `raw_snapshot_count` — all of which move without the evidence moving. Because the observation vector stores each element's weight **anchored to the newest price event** rather than to `now` or to the newest raw snapshot (§3.3.4, S2-25), and because an element's `event_at` is a historical run start rather than a window-relative position, the digest is constant across ticks for a fixed observation set — which is what makes "quiet system, zero writes" true rather than aspirational. Revision 4's `max(observed_at)` anchor did not have that property: it advanced on every poll of every member, so every element's `w_rel` moved and the digest with it.

If a future version introduces a statistic that is genuinely not scale-invariant — an absolute weight sum, a calendar-anchored decay, or an absolute-currency threshold — a time trigger has to come back with it. That is the condition that reopens this decision, and it is recorded here so a later author does not reintroduce B7 by reflex.

**B. Listing rescore triggers.** A listing in `P` (§2.2) is rescored when **any** of the following holds:

| # | Trigger | Reaches |
| --- | --- | --- |
| L1 | Its cohort's current baseline **version** changed (`current.current_version_id ≠ the version the score cites`) | Every listing in that cohort |
| L2 | Its **canonical input fingerprint** changed (below) | That listing |
| L3 | Its `resolution_grain` changed, or its current `listing_resolution` id changed at the same grain | That listing (and, via B5, its old and new cohorts) |
| L4 | A `SellerRatingObservation` was written for its seller | Every listing in `P` for that seller |
| L5 | `SourceConfig.seller_policy_prior` changed for its source | Every listing in `P` for that source |
| L6 | It re-entered `P` (relist, or a snapshot pushing `expires_at` forward) | That listing — it has no current decision digest, so §3.4.2 always writes |
| L7 | `algorithm_version` bump | The entire population (a full rescore, S2-6) |
| L8 | The snapshot `S(listing)` names was deleted or expired, so §2.2.1's selector re-evaluates | That listing — driven from the sweep's own deleted keys, not from a re-read, because the current facts move *backwards* in time (§2.2.1) |
| L9 | **The selected `SellerRatingObservation` was deleted or expired**, so §3.2.5(d)'s selection re-evaluates | Every listing in `P` for that seller — driven from the sweep's deleted keys for the same reason as L8: the seller aggregate moves *backwards* to an older surviving row, or to the S2-12 prior, and no fingerprint re-read is scheduled to notice (review finding SA-022) |

L4 and L5 are covered mechanically by L2 once the seller aggregate and the prior are part of the fingerprint; they are listed separately because the **fan-out** differs and the MS-2d test must exercise each fan-out shape independently.

**L9 and its sweep hook (review finding SA-022).** `SellerRatingObservation` is `RetentionGoverned` and eBay-sourced rows carry `ebay_listing_observation`, so the sweep deletes them on the ordinary six-hour clock — and `purge_expired` deletes every expired bounded model **in independently selected batches and clears nothing on any parent** (verified at `catalog/management/commands/purge_expired.py:186-223`). L4 fires only on a *write*, so before revision 5 a listing could keep a seller trust value, a cap outcome and an explanation clause derived from a rating row that no longer existed.

- **The hook.** `SellerRatingObservation.invalidate_seller_scores(deleted_keys)` — a classmethod discovered and dispatched by the sweep exactly the way it already discovers `deletion_exempt_q` and `redact_expired` (`purge_expired.py:112-168`, `getattr(model, ...)`), called **inside the same per-model transaction as the delete**, taking the deleted `(seller_id, observed_at)` pairs. It is the seller-side sibling of `ListingScore.clear_parent_denorm(deleted_keys)` from §2.3(b), and `OfferSnapshot.invalidate_listing_scores(deleted_keys)` is the L8 sibling of both; MS-2a lands all three under one hook protocol so the sweep gains no per-model special cases.
- **What it does — no queue, per S2-4.** It clears the `current_*` columns (§2.1) of every listing in `P` for the affected sellers. A listing with no `current_scored_at` has no current decision digest, so §3.4.2 always writes for it and the refresh's candidate set picks it up on the next tick (the same mechanism L6 already relies on for a relisted listing). No `score_recompute_queue` table is introduced, which is what S2-4 rules out.
- **Conditional, like §2.3(b)'s clear.** The clear applies only when the deleted pair **is** the seller's currently selected observation — a batch that deletes an older, unselected row changes `aggregate(seller)` not at all and must not cost a fan-out. The guard is the same shape as the `AND current_scored_at = %(scored_at)s` predicate: clear only where the score's stored group-3 `observed_at` equals a deleted row's.
- **The post-delete aggregate is §3.2.5(d) re-run over the survivors**: the greatest-`observed_at` surviving row **whatever its usability**, and the S2-12 source prior when none survives. There is no separate rule here — that is the point of making selection unconditional in SA-016.

**Tests (MS-2d, live DB — SA-022's `suggested_validation`):** score five listings of one eBay seller against a usable rating; expire that rating; run `purge_expired`; assert **every** one of the five is rescored **exactly once**, that the new aggregate is the next surviving row (in one case) or the S2-12 prior with `seller_no_rating` (in a second case where no row survives), and that the seller's cap outcome and explanation change accordingly. Negative controls: deleting a **non-selected** older row rescores **nothing**; a second sweep with nothing to delete writes nothing; a seller with no listings in `P` produces no fan-out.

**The canonical dependency document.** Review finding SA-003, second half: revision 2 enumerated *derived* values (normalized quantity, capacity, bases) in the fingerprint, so an extraction change that left every normalized value alone — a corrected `source_text`, a re-tuned confidence, a different producing layer — did not have to trigger recomputation, and the current row kept explaining itself with evidence that no longer existed. The fix is to stop maintaining two lists.

`D(listing)` is **one canonical dependency document**: the complete, ordered set of persisted facts the decision consumes, each carried **with its provenance record rather than only its normalized value**. It has exactly four groups:

1. **Offer facts** — taken from **`S(listing)`, the §2.2.1 current offer snapshot**, and from no other row: `usd_total_landed_price`, `total_landed_price`, source currency, `fx_rate`, `stock_status`, and that snapshot's `observed_at`. Revision 3 said "the snapshot they came from" without saying which one; §2.2.1 (decision S2-22) is now the rule, and because it selects by greatest `observed_at` among eligible rows, a late-arriving older snapshot changes nothing in this group (review finding SA-018).
2. **Extraction provenance** — the full `Listing.extracted_attrs_json` contents verbatim: for **every** N2 attribute present, the complete `{value, confidence, layer, source_text}` record from `matching/types.py` `Attribute[T]`, not the value alone. This is the group revision 2 was missing, and it is why a provenance-only change now produces a new decision.
3. **Authoritative catalog and seller facts** — `capacity_tb_per_unit`, `condition`, `recert_channel`, `packaging`, `returns_policy` + `returns_window_days`, `recording_tech` (including its NULL state), `market_tier`, canonical `interface`, canonical `form_factor`, `media_type`, `dwpd`, `warranty_channel` + `warranty_months`, the **§3.2.5 seller aggregate** as of scoring — the normalized `p_obs`, `n`, `y`, `count_basis`, `is_usable` and the `observed_at` of the single selected `SellerRatingObservation` (never a sum, never an average; revision 3's `positive_count` / `total_count` pair is withdrawn with the model fields, review finding SA-016) — `seller_policy_prior`, and the current `listing_resolution` id + `grain` + `matcher_version`.
4. **The baseline evidence snapshot** (review finding SA-015, renamed in revision 5) — the cohort context plus everything the price subscore **and its explanation** were computed from: `cohort_key`, `relaxation_step`, `relaxation_exhausted`, the baseline's **`baseline_digest`**, and **`q`**, **`n_eff`**, **`lambda_shrinkage`**, this listing's own **`ln_price_per_tb`** and **`w_rel`**, its **`in_baseline`** membership flag, the weighted **`q25` / `q50` / `q75`**, **`margin_iqr`**, and **`source_composition_rendered`** (§3.3.5d gives the field-by-field justification). Deliberately **not** the baseline row id and **not** its `computed_at`: those are recomputation identity, not semantics, and including them is exactly what made revision 2's hourly refresh append a row per listing (SA-014).

   The snapshot is what makes a historical score re-derivable after its cohort has moved on — §3.3.5(d) states why a digest alone is not evidence and what the reproduction test asserts. Its members are **inputs to this row's decision**, recorded as such, not derived outputs: `q` and the quartiles cannot be recomputed from anything else the row stores once the baseline version's vector is out of reach, which is precisely the state the reviewer's test creates. Their presence in `D` also means a cohort change that moves this listing's `q` or its margin moves its `inputs_digest` directly, rather than only through `baseline_digest`.

   On a neutral path (§3.3.5c) the cohort consumed no evidence, so the snapshot degenerates rather than disappearing: `q`, `n_eff`, `λ`, `margin_iqr` and the quartiles are NULL, `in_baseline` is false, `baseline_digest` is NULL, `source_composition_rendered` is empty, and `price_basis` states which of the three paths applied. "No evidence" is recorded as a value, not as an absent key, so the canonicalization is stable and the explanation regenerates on those rows too.

`ListingScore.inputs_digest = SHA-256(canonical_json(D))` under the §3.4.2 canonicalization, and **`ListingScore.inputs_json` is `D` itself** — the row stores the exact document it was digested from, so NFR-007 reproduction reads one field rather than reassembling inputs from a paraphrase. Derived values (`dollars_per_tb`, `quantity`, `quantity_basis`, `price_basis`) are outputs recorded on the row and covered by §3.4.2's decision digest; they are not members of `D`, because digesting both a fact and its own derivation double-counts the fact and hides which one actually moved.

**The one deliberate exception, and the line it draws.** Group 4's values *are* computed rather than read from a row, so they look like the derived outputs the rule above excludes. **Naming the group the "baseline evidence snapshot" is the resolution, not a euphemism**: the distinction is **where the fact they summarize lives**. `dollars_per_tb` is derived from facts `D` already carries in full (price, capacity, quantity), so storing it too would double-count them; every group-4 value summarizes the baseline **version's observation vector**, which `D` deliberately does *not* carry — group 4 holds only its digest. Without them the vector is the fact and the score retains none of it, which is exactly the SA-015 gap, in both its round-3 form (the number) and its round-4 form (the explanation). The rule therefore stands, stated once for the whole document:

> `D` carries every fact the decision consumed. Where the underlying fact is itself retained, `D` carries the fact and never its derivation. Where the underlying fact is **not** retained — today, only the baseline version's observation vector — `D` carries the retained projection of it, and that projection is group 4.

`price_basis` stays an output, because every fact that determines it (capacity presence, snapshot eligibility, cohort emptiness) is already in `D`. `margin_iqr` is in group 4 rather than being an output for the opposite reason: the quartiles it divides by are not otherwise retained anywhere the score can reach.

Because group 4 carries `baseline_digest`, L1 is implied by L2 — but the refresh evaluates L1 first as a set operation, so a semantically changed baseline rescores its cohort in one query rather than by fingerprinting every listing individually. Conversely, a baseline recomputation that produces the same `baseline_digest` changes no listing's `D`, so it writes nothing anywhere.

**`CohortBaseline.member_projection_digest`** (the B5 stamp; revision 4's `member_resolution_digest` is **withdrawn** — review finding SA-023). Revision 4 hashed `[listing_id, current listing_resolution id]` pairs, which is blind to everything below model grain: §3.3.3 makes **extracted** attributes decide capacity bucket, interface/form, condition and `$/TB` for an unresolved listing, so those values can change — a re-tuned extractor, a corrected title, a new quantity reading — while the resolution id stays identical, moving the listing's observations between cohorts with neither cohort marked stale. The digest is therefore taken over the **cohort-shaping projection** of each member:

```text
π(listing) := [ listing_id,
                current listing_resolution id, resolution grain,
                capacity_tb_per_unit, interface, form_factor,      -- canonical tokens (§3.3.1)
                market_tier, condition, recert_channel,
                quantity, quantity_basis,                          -- §3.2.2
                provenance layer of each of the above ]            -- authoritative vs. extracted (§3.3.3)
member_projection_digest(K) := SHA-256(canonical_json(sorted by listing_id [ π(l) for l in members(K) ]))
```

- **The projection carries provenance, not just values**, for the same reason group 2 of `D` does (SA-003): a value that stays the same while its layer changes is a different fact about the cohort, and it is exactly the case a value-only digest hides.
- **Both the old and the new cohort are dirtied, structurally.** B5 is evaluated over the **union** of (i) every `cohort_baseline_current.cohort_key` and (ii) every cohort key present in the freshly computed grouped projection, with the missing side taken as the **empty digest**. A listing that moves from `K_old` to `K_new` therefore changes `K_old`'s digest (it lost a member — and if it was the last member, `K_old` compares against the empty projection rather than being skipped for having no group row) **and** `K_new`'s digest (gained, or newly created). No per-listing history is needed to reach the old cohort, which is what makes this total rather than best-effort.
- **How the old key is known when it is needed by name.** The grouped union above is the mechanism; `Listing.current_cohort_key` (§2.1) is the *recorded* last-scored key, and it is what the refresh logs, what `detail_json` reports for a moved listing, and what an operator's targeted `manage.py score_refresh --force-cohort <key>` takes. §2.3(b) states why clearing it does not weaken the union rule. The latest `ListingScore.cohort_key` is deliberately **not** the source of truth for this: eBay score rows expire under S2-2a, so that history is not guaranteed to exist.
- **Detection versus authority, unchanged.** B5 stays a **cheap detection shortcut evaluated before the 90-day pull** and is deliberately not the authority on whether to write: `baseline_digest` is that authority, and a B5 hit whose recomputed `baseline_digest` matches produces no write and only advances `last_evaluated_at`. Keeping the two separate is what lets detection be conservative (over-fire freely) without costing a write — which matters more now, because the projection changes more often than a resolution id does.

**Tests (MS-2d, live DB — SA-023's `suggested_validation`):** for a **below-model-grain** listing whose resolution id is held constant, change **only** extracted `capacity`, then only `interface`, then only `condition`, then only `quantity` — four separate cases — and in each assert that the **old** and the **new** cohort's `member_projection_digest` both change, that both baselines are refreshed, that exactly the member listings of both cohorts are rescored, and that no third cohort is touched. A fifth case changes only a provenance layer with every value identical and asserts the digest still moves; a sixth moves the last member out of a cohort and asserts the emptied cohort is compared against the empty projection and refreshed rather than silently skipped.

**Delist, expiry, and redaction are *not* rescore triggers.** A listing leaving `P` is not rescored; its denormalized fields are cleared per §2.3(b) and its history is left to its retention class.

**C. How the refresh actually finds the affected listings.** The triggers above say *when* a listing must be rescored; this says how a 60 s tick computes that set without a queue (S2-4) and without fingerprinting the whole population. Each clause is one indexed query, and the union is the tick's candidate set:

It is the correctness backstop, not the cheap path. MS-2d measures it against the S2-3 60 s budget; if it dominates, the named tunable is `B3_INTERVAL` (a constant under S2-6, proposed **5 minutes**) so B3 runs on a subset of ticks while the indexed triggers run on all of them — a bounded detection *lag*, never a missed change, because a digest mismatch does not expire. Lowering B1's coverage is **not** an available tunable.

#### 3.4.2 Write-on-change, keyed on the whole decision

Review finding SA-006, blocking: appending a row only when `deal_score`, the cap set, `is_provisional`, or `algorithm_version` changed leaves the current row describing obsolete inputs whenever a subscore, `$/TB`, cohort key, matcher grain, risk flag, seller evidence, margin, or explanation text moves without shifting the rounded score. DR-004 requires the stored row to be the most recent automated decision, not merely a matching number.

**Rule.** `ListingScore.decision_digest` is `SHA-256` over the canonical JSON of the **complete DR-004 decision payload**: `inputs_json` (which itself contains everything the §3.4.1 fingerprint covers, plus provenance records), all four subscores, `base_score`, `cap_applied` and `cap_reasons`, `deal_score`, `dollars_per_tb`, `price_basis`, `quantity` and `quantity_basis`, `n_eff`, `lambda_shrinkage`, `is_provisional`, `provisional_reasons`, `cohort_key`, the `cohort_baseline` **version** id (§3.3.5 — it moves exactly when `baseline_digest` moves, so it adds no write amplification), `relaxation_step`, `relaxation_exhausted`, `matcher_grain`, `risk_flags`, thresholds and margins, `explanation_json`, `explanation_text`, and `algorithm_version`.

A new row is appended **iff** the freshly computed `decision_digest` differs from the current row's. Zero writes occur **only** when the complete canonical decision is byte-identical.

Canonicalization is pinned in `scoring/digest.py`: sorted keys, UTF-8, no insignificant whitespace, and **all floats quantized to 6 decimal places before serialization**. Without the quantization, ordinary floating-point noise would defeat idempotence.

**What actually makes a quiet system write nothing** (corrected in revision 3, review finding SA-014): not a staleness ceiling — that ceiling was the write amplifier, not the cure — but the two invariance properties above it. Between real dependency changes the decay weights differ only by a common positive factor, so `n_eff`, `λ`, `q` and the quantiles are numerically identical tick to tick; the §3.4.1 `baseline_digest` excludes recomputation identity so an unchanged cohort produces an unchanged digest; and `D` carries that digest rather than the baseline's row id, so no listing's `inputs_digest` moves either. Quantization then absorbs the last few ulps of floating-point noise. Nothing in this chain depends on how often the job ticks, which is why the 60 s interval of S2-3 is affordable.

**Tests (MS-2d):** change each explanation-only field in turn (a risk flag; the seller evidence counts; `dollars_per_tb` by a sub-rounding amount; the cohort key via relaxation; `matcher_grain`) while holding `deal_score`, the cap set, `is_provisional` and `algorithm_version` constant, and assert a new row **and** an updated `listing.current_*` pointer. Then repeat with a genuinely identical canonical payload and assert **zero** writes and an untouched `current_scored_at`.

#### 3.4.3 Immutable version dispatch (reproducing history)

Review finding SA-010; decision S2-13. Storing `algorithm_version` proves *which* algorithm ran; it does not keep that algorithm runnable. Once a constant changes and the version bumps, a golden test over the current version passes while every older row becomes underivable from the tree — and NFR-007 asks for reproducibility, not for a value in a column.

**Contract:**

Review round 3 found this contract still only **partially** resolved (SA-010). Revision 3 froze each version *directory* and protected its manifest entry against merge-base edits, which closed the shared-helper and editable-manifest holes. It left three doors open, all outside the frozen directory: historical execution still passed through the mutable `scoring/versions/__init__.py` registry, through `scoring/compose.py`'s wrapper behavior, and through `scoring/contracts.py`'s parsing, coercion and defaults — so changing dispatch or an input default could alter or disable a historical version while every directory digest still matched. Retirement was also proven only against "a management command's database", which an empty CI or development database satisfies vacuously. Items 1, 2 and 5 below close all three. **Round 4 then found two of them still open** (SA-010): revision 4's item 2 protected the registry but still executed through `compose.py`, and its item 5 checked only the *latest* backup while §18.6 retains six months of restorable dumps. Revision 5 closes both, in the same two items.

1. **Each released version is a package directory with a self-contained entry point, and nothing score-shaping is left outside it.** `scoring/versions/v<N>/` holds the complete constant set, every score-shaping helper it uses, **and its own parsing**. Its public surface is exactly one callable:

   ```python
   # Binding — the only cross-boundary contract a released version exposes.
   def score(D: dict) -> dict: ...
   ```

   `D` is the §3.4.1 canonical dependency document as **plain JSON-compatible data** — the same bytes `inputs_json` stores — and the return value is plain data too. **The version parses `D` itself**, with version-local validation, coercion and defaults; a field added to `D` after `v<N>` was released is ignored by `v<N>` because `v<N>`'s own parser does not know it, which is the behavior NFR-007 wants. Concretely, code under `v<N>/` may import only (a) the Python standard library and (b) other modules inside its own `v<N>/` package. **It may not import `scoring/contracts.py`, `stats.py`, `subscores.py`, `vetoes.py`, `cohort.py`, `digest.py`, `explain.py`, or any other shared module** — revision 3 permitted `contracts.py` on the grounds that it holds "data shapes with no score-shaping behavior", and that was the remaining hole: a Pydantic model carries validators, coercion rules and field defaults, so a default added there changes what a historical version computes for an input outside the golden fixtures. `scoring/contracts.py` is now the **current version's public façade only** — the typed shape MS-3 and the service layer consume — and the shared `stats.py` / `subscores.py` / `vetoes.py` / `cohort.py` / `explain.py` modules become thin current-version wrappers that delegate into the dispatched package. The duplication between `v1/` and `v2/` is the point: a released version's behavior stops depending on anything that can still be edited.

2. **The registry is static data, its digest is part of every version's manifest entry, and historical execution does not pass through `compose.py` at all.** `scoring/versions/__init__.py` is a plain mapping literal `ALGORITHM_VERSIONS = {"v1": v1, ...}` with no conditionals, no aliasing, no fallback and no default; an unknown `algorithm_version` raises rather than resolving to the newest.

   Round 4 found the remaining hole (SA-010): revision 4 still routed every call through `scoring/compose.py`, a mutable module outside every protected digest, which can preprocess `D` or postprocess the result while the manifest gate stays green. **The dispatcher therefore resolves the package from the registry and calls `ALGORITHM_VERSIONS[v].score(D)` directly.** Concretely:

   ```python
   # Binding — scoring/dispatch.py, the whole historical execution path.
   def score_with(version: str, D: dict) -> dict:
       return ALGORITHM_VERSIONS[version].score(D)   # no transform before, none after
   ```

   `scoring/dispatch.py` may contain **only** that lookup-and-call: no defaulting, no coercion, no merging, no post-processing, no logging of a transformed payload. `scoring/compose.py` becomes the **current-version façade only** — it builds `D` for a *new* score and returns the typed `contracts.py` shape MS-3 consumes — and it is never on the path of a historical re-derivation, which reads `inputs_json` and calls `score_with` directly. *Rejected alternative:* folding `compose.py` into every version's manifest digest. It would work, but it freezes a module whose whole purpose is to move with the current version, so every future release would have to fork it; and it leaves the wrapper *on* the historical path, which means the protection is a hash rather than an absence. Removing the module from the path is the stronger property: there is nothing to protect.

   The gate enforces the boundary the same way it enforces the import rule: `scripts.check` fails if `scoring/dispatch.py` contains any statement other than the registry lookup and the call, and fails if any code path that re-derives a stored score imports `compose.py`. `MANIFEST.json` records, per released version, **two** digests: the **directory digest** (SHA-256 over the canonical JSON of every file's repository-relative path and content hash under `v<N>/`, sorted by path — so adding, deleting or renaming a file changes it exactly as editing one does) **and the `registry_digest`**, the content hash of `versions/__init__.py`. Because the registry digest is recorded in *every* entry, **any** edit to the dispatch mapping invalidates **every** released entry at once, and the only way back to green is to write the new registry digest into each entry — which the merge-base rule below rejects for existing entries. Adding a `v(N+1)` therefore requires adding its entry *and* rewriting each existing entry's `registry_digest`, so the sanctioned path is explicit: the gate accepts an existing entry whose **only** difference from the merge base is a `registry_digest` update that matches the new registry, and rejects every other difference. That is the narrow, stated exception; without it the "additions only" rule would make adding a version impossible, and an author would end up loosening the rule instead.

   `scripts.check` enforces, against the manifest as it stands in the **merge base**:
   - an **existing** entry whose directory digest differs from the merge-base manifest is a **failure**, even when the package and its entry were edited together — this is the case revision 2 let through;
   - an existing entry whose recomputed directory digest differs from its recorded digest is a **failure**;
   - an existing entry whose `registry_digest` differs from the merge base **and** does not equal the current `versions/__init__.py` hash is a **failure**; equal is accepted only when the registry change is purely additive (every merge-base key still maps to the same package) — a key that is **removed or repointed** fails regardless;
   - the **import-boundary check**: any import inside `v<N>/` outside the standard library and its own package is a **failure**;
   - **additions are the only other sanctioned change**, and a **removal** fails unless the version appears in an explicit `retired` allowlist in the same manifest, which item 5's proof is what justifies adding to.

3. **Every version's golden fixtures run on every change** — `tests/golden/scoring/v<N>/` for each released `N`, not just the current one. With items 1 and 2 in force these fixtures are a **regression net, not the immutability mechanism**: a shared-helper edit, a parsing default, and a registry repoint can no longer reach a released version at all, so the fixtures no longer have to be complete enough to catch them. That inversion is the substance of the SA-010 fix — revision 2 asked finite fixtures to prove an infinite property.
4. The constant set a version owns: the four component weights, the `0.02` subscore floor, the three cap values, the veto trigger predicates, the half-life and window, the `λ` target of 30, the seller prior `μ₀`/`κ` and Wilson `z`, the fitness sub-weights and rubric tables, the availability rubric's interior values, `CAPACITY_BUCKETS_TB` and its 5% tolerance, the parent-tier map, `QUANTITY_MIN_CONFIDENCE`, `MAX_LOT_QUANTITY`, the rounding mode, and the digest canonicalization rules.
5. **Retirement is an operational procedure, and CI cannot perform it.** Revision 3 said "a management command shows no `listing_score` row cites it" without saying *which database*, and an empty CI or development database answers "none" for every version — so the check could pass while production and every restorable backup still held citing `merchant_fact` rows. Retirement is therefore defined as a **runbook step against every authoritative dataset**, not a gate:

   - The **authoritative datasets** are (i) the production database (`docs/handoff/deployed.md`) and (ii) **every retained restorable backup** — master-spec §18.6 retains **48 hourly, 14 daily, 8 weekly and 6 monthly** dumps, and a restore of *any* of them reintroduces the rows it holds. Revision 4 said "the latest restorable backup", which review finding SA-010 correctly reads as leaving up to six months of restorable citations unchecked: retire a version today and restore last month's dump tomorrow, and the restored scores are underivable.
   - **The retirement rule, stated as a date comparison rather than a scan.** Restoring every retained dump to prove a negative is not operationally reasonable, and it is not necessary. Let `t_last` be the instant the **last citing row** left the production database (its deletion or expiry, recorded when the operator observes the zero count), and let `t_oldest` be the `pg_dump` timestamp of the **oldest retained dump** — under the §18.6 schedule, up to six months old. Then:

     ```text
     retirable(v)  ⇔  production citations(v) = 0
                      AND t_oldest > t_last          -- every retained dump was taken after the last citation was gone
     ```

     A version is therefore not retirable until the retention schedule has rolled the last citing dump out of the set — in the worst case six months after the last citing row was deleted. The rule is a comparison of two recorded timestamps, so it is auditable from the PR without restoring anything; a restore is required only when the operator cannot establish `t_last` from the production record and must fall back to citation counts against individually restored dumps.
   - `manage.py scoring_version_citations --database-url <URL> --version v<N>` runs the citation count **against a named database URL** and prints the count, the dataset it was run against, and the timestamp. It takes no default and refuses to run against the configured application database implicitly, so "which database" is always recorded rather than assumed. The URL is supplied from the environment or the operator's secret store and is **never** written into this repository, a plan, or a runbook — only the variable name is (public-repo rule).
   - Retirement is **refused** while production reports a nonzero count, and refused while `t_oldest ≤ t_last`. The operator records in the PR that adds the `retired` allowlist entry: the production count and its timestamp, `t_last` with the evidence for it, `t_oldest` and the backup-set identifiers it came from, and any individually restored dump they had to fall back to. The gate then checks only that the allowlist entry exists; the manifest entry preserves the recorded counts, timestamps and dataset identifiers.
   - **`scripts.check` cannot prove retirement and does not try.** It has no access to production or to a restored backup, and giving CI that access to satisfy a hygiene check would be a far worse trade than keeping a version directory. The gate's job is to make deletion *impossible without the allowlist entry*; the allowlist entry's job is to record that a human ran the proof.
   - Because bounded eBay `listing_score` rows expire under S2-2a while `merchant_fact` rows do not, a version cited by any non-eBay score is effectively permanent. That is the intended outcome, and §3.3.5(f) makes the same statement about baseline versions.

*Rejected alternative:* a per-row reproducibility manifest embedding the constant set in `inputs_json`. Rejected because the constants are only half the algorithm: the rubric structure, the relaxation order, the rounding mode and the digest canonicalization are code, not values, and a row cannot carry them. A manifest would make the constants auditable while still leaving the score underivable — the appearance of NFR-007 compliance without the substance.

**Tests (MS-2d) — each of the three SA-010 escape routes gets its own negative control:**

Add an import from `scoring/stats.py` inside `v1/`, and separately from `scoring/contracts.py`, and assert the import check fails for both.

#### 3.4.4 The global-run schema (`ScoringRun`)

Review finding SA-013, blocking. Revision 2 said the refresh is explicitly **cross-source** and then proposed to record it as `RunKind.SCORE` on `ScraperRun`. That is not implementable: `ScraperRun.source_site` is a non-null `PROTECT` foreign key to `SourceSite` (`catalog/models/ops.py:206-225`), so every row must name one merchant. The three ways out of that were a nullable `source_site`, a sentinel non-merchant `SourceSite` row, and a separate model. **Decision S2-19 takes the separate model.**

*Alternatives rejected.* **Nullable `source_site`** — it weakens an invariant that currently holds for every acquisition run, and it silently changes the meaning of the existing `("source_site", "-started_at")` index and of every source-health query that assumes a run has a source; the cost lands on MS-1 code that has nothing to do with scoring. **A sentinel `SourceSite`** — a fake merchant in a table whose rows carry retention class, licensing posture, and poll cadence is exactly the "invent a fake source" failure the reviewer named, and it would appear in operator source lists and cadence reports as a source that cannot be polled. A separate model costs one migration and buys both invariants intact.

**Shape.** `ScoringRun` in `catalog/models/ops.py`, a plain `models.Model` alongside `ScraperRun` (operational telemetry only — it holds counts, not merchant content, so it is deliberately **not** `RetentionGoverned`, matching `ScraperRun`'s own posture):

| Field | Type | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `started_at` | `DateTimeField` | Set when the refresh begins |
| `finished_at` | `DateTimeField(null=True, blank=True)` | NULL while running or after a hard crash |
| `status` | `CharField(choices=RunStatus.choices, default=RUNNING)` | **Reuses** the existing `RunStatus` |
| `failure_class` | `CharField(choices=RunFailureClass.choices, blank=True, default="")` | **Reuses** the existing `RunFailureClass` |
| `algorithm_version` | `CharField` | Which version this run executed |
| `cohorts_evaluated` | `PositiveIntegerField(default=0)` | Cohorts whose dirtiness was checked |
| `cohorts_refreshed` | `PositiveIntegerField(default=0)` | Cohorts that were actually recomputed (B1–B6 fired) |
| `baseline_writes` | `PositiveIntegerField(default=0)` | Baselines whose `baseline_digest` actually changed |
| `listings_rescored` | `PositiveIntegerField(default=0)` | Listings whose score was recomputed |
| `score_writes` | `PositiveIntegerField(default=0)` | New `listing_score` rows appended (§3.4.2) |
| `baseline_versions_pruned` | `PositiveIntegerField(default=0)` | Superseded, uncited baseline versions reclaimed by the §3.3.5(g) sweep; zero on ticks where the sweep did not run |
| `error` | `TextField(blank=True, default="")` | Same shape as `ScraperRun.error` |
| `detail_json` | `JSONField(default=dict, blank=True)` | Per-cohort failure detail for the isolation path |

`Meta.db_table = "scoring_run"`, one index on `("-started_at",)`. There is **no** `source_site` field and no run-kind discriminator — the model *is* the discriminator.

The counts are what make the SA-014 no-drift claim auditable in production rather than only in a test: on a quiet tick `cohorts_evaluated` is positive while `cohorts_refreshed`, `baseline_writes`, `listings_rescored` and `score_writes` are all zero. `baseline_versions_pruned` is reported separately because pruning is storage hygiene, not scoring work, and a nonzero value on an otherwise quiet tick is expected rather than a drift signal. Together with §3.3.5(h), the pair `baseline_writes` / `baseline_versions_pruned` is the production measurement of baseline-version growth.

**Source-health queries are unaffected by construction.** Every cadence, lane, freshness and failure-rate query in MS-1 reads `ScraperRun` (or `SourceLaneState`), and `ScoringRun` is a different table with no relation to `SourceSite`, so no existing query can see a scoring run and none needs a `run_kind != score` filter. That is the concrete advantage over the nullable and sentinel options, both of which would have required auditing every such query.

**Tests (MS-2d, live DB):**

1. Persist a **successful** `ScoringRun` with no merchant source at all; assert the row saves, `status = success`, `finished_at` set, and the six counts equal the refresh's actual work.
2. Persist a **failed** `ScoringRun` with a `RunFailureClass`; assert it saves with no source and that a per-cohort failure leaves `status = success` with the failure recorded in `detail_json` (the §4 isolation posture), while a whole-run failure sets `status = failed`.
3. **Negative control on the untouched invariant:** constructing a `ScraperRun` without `source_site` still raises; existing acquisition runs continue to require a source.
4. **Non-interference:** run one scoring refresh, then assert every source-health and cadence query returns exactly what it returned before the refresh — the scoring run is invisible to them.

## 4. Error handling & ops (MS-2 posture)

- The `score-refresh` job writes one **`ScoringRun`** row per run (§3.4.4, S2-19) — status, cohorts evaluated and refreshed, baseline writes, listings rescored, score writes, runtime, failure class (NFR-004). Scoring becomes visible in the same operational place acquisition already is, **without** borrowing `ScraperRun`'s per-source shape.
- A per-cohort failure is isolated (MS-2d); a total refresh failure surfaces as a failed `ScoringRun` and, from MS-4, an operator alert. **No email alerts until MS-4** (ADR 0013) — the MS-1 posture is unchanged: DB rows, structured logs, and the §18.5 dead-man's switch.
- `max_instances=1` + `coalesce=True` are already the poller's `job_defaults`; scoring inherits them, so a slow refresh degrades to lower frequency rather than to concurrent double-writes. §14's "single user; `max_instances=1` per job; no locking/queueing design needed" holds for scoring too.
- **Capacity watch:** `listing_score` **and the §3.3.5 append-only `cohort_baseline` versions** are new write paths onto the same disk as `offer_snapshot` and the raw payloads. The §3.4.2 decision digest is the whole bound, and revision 3 makes it a real one: with B7 withdrawn (SA-014) a quiet 60 s tick performs bounded detection queries and writes nothing at all, instead of appending a row per affected listing every hour. Growth is now proportional to genuine input change, which is the property the storage rationale always claimed. The MS-1d disk-space alert (CT-116) covers the growth, and the TimescaleDB-aware dump pipeline must be confirmed to include the new tables before the first production refresh — the same pre-real-data check MS-1d ran, re-verified rather than assumed. **Baseline versions are the second bound and the less certain one:** §3.3.5(h) derives an order-10¹ MB/day estimate at v1 scale and makes the MS-2c measurement a precondition for enabling MS-2d in production, with the §3.3.5(g) prune sweep and the `baseline_versions_pruned` count as the ongoing evidence. **Revision 5 changes what that number rests on rather than the number itself** (review finding SA-021): revision 4 derived it from price events while its own definition of `E` produced a version per **poll**, i.e. order 10³ MB/day on an unchanging system — so §4 was carrying an estimate the mechanism could not deliver. The S2-25 event definition makes the version rate proportional to actual price movement, which is what the estimate always assumed. It remains a derivation from this document's own assumed cohort sizes, not a measurement, and it is still the weakest number in §4 until MS-2c reports.
- **Refresh cost has a scan in it, and it is named:** B3's grouped `price_event_digest` query is the only unindexed step of the dirtiness pass (§3.4.1C). MS-2d records its share of the 60 s budget; the tunable if it dominates is `B3_INTERVAL`, which lags detection without losing it. B1's ingestion high-water is what keeps the *refresh* off that path in the common case, and its §2.2.2 commit-lag margin means a just-arrived snapshot can be one tick late — an acceptable trade against a permanently skipped row.
- **Version retirement is an operator runbook step, not a job and not a gate** (§3.4.3 item 5): the citation proof runs against the production database and against **the whole retained backup set** (master-spec §18.6 — 48 hourly, 14 daily, 8 weekly, 6 monthly), reduced to the `t_oldest > t_last` date comparison so no operator has to restore six months of dumps to prove a negative. `scripts.check` deliberately has access to none of them. The practical consequence, stated because it is a real cost: **a version stays on disk for up to six months after its last citing row is deleted**, because that is how long a dump that still cites it remains restorable. A version whose directory still exists costs kilobytes; a version deleted while a restorable backup still cites it costs the reproducibility NFR-007 asks for.
- An `algorithm_version` bump triggers a full rescore; at v1 scale that is a single long refresh, not a migration, and it is announced in the sub-milestone PR that bumps it.
- The §2.3(b) clearing hooks run inside existing transactions; a crash between the delist mark and the clear is impossible by construction, and the MS-2a test asserts the two cannot be observed separately.

## 7. Execution process
